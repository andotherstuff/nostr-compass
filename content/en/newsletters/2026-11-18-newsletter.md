Several projects put tighter limits on encrypted media and keys this week. [Applesauce](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/releases/tag/applesauce-extra%407.2.0) bounds attachment downloads, [MDK](#marmot-protocol-mdk) changes recovery, device and media handling in source, and [Keep Android](#privkeyio-keep-android) changes how it trusts relay certificates. Clients and wallets, including [Wisp](#barrydeen-wisp) and ZEUS, fix reliability problems. [Hashtree](#mmalmi-hashtree) releases browser read and indexing changes, while its paid blob retrieval exists only in source. Protocol experiments such as [Scheduled Tasks](#nostr-protocol-nips-2546) and [NAP-WEBRTC](#napplet-naps-145) are still open proposals or branch-only work. The [deep dive](#nip-deep-dive-public-goals-and-peer-to-peer-orders) covers public zap goals and peer-to-peer orders.

## Top stories

### Sidecar {#dmnyc-sidecar}

Sidecar is a browser side-panel Nostr client with a built-in signer. [v1.17.1](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/releases/tag/v1.17.1) adds one relay-backed library for bookmarks, highlights, web comments and reactions. It includes bookmark sets created in other clients: Sidecar reads kinds 10003, 30003 and 30001, uses the owner's key to decrypt private entries over [NIP-44](/en/topics/nip-44/) or [NIP-04](/en/topics/nip-04/), and shows private entries as read-only. Web comments appear as kind 1111 reply trees. Users can add a custom client by pasting a sample note or profile URL. Sidecar parses the link, rebuilds it and saves the client only when the rebuilt link matches the original, and it removes relay hints from `nprofile` links. Much of this work was merged in [#612](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/pull/612) and [#621](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/pull/621). Because the library holds only what the queried relays return, it may not include a user's complete history.

In [v1.17.2](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/releases/tag/v1.17.2), [livestream chat](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/blob/v1.17.2/stream-chat.js) can be read before an account is added. Writing actions are hidden, controls that need an account are disabled and the sign-up link opens Accounts. Locked panels still hide chat. Reply and mention names can now resolve from the target's own relays. [#627](https://github.com/dmnyc/sidecar/pull/627) separates approval of a single signature from a per-kind grant, which Sidecar offers after three requests of the same kind. Users can also choose a time-limited approval window, and sites that share an account get no standing grants. Choosing an account applies to that one signature and leaves the active account and the site pin unchanged. Wallet, deletion, authentication and list events remain excluded from grants.

### Formstr nostr-docs {#formstr-hq-nostr-docs}

Formstr's nostr-docs, an encrypted collaborative document editor built on Nostr, merged a sharing rework that separates the right to decrypt a document from permission to edit it ([#82](https://github.com/formstr-hq/nostr-docs/pull/82), [#79](https://github.com/formstr-hq/nostr-docs/pull/79)). Restricted shares accept writes from the owner and invited editors. Older documents keep working through a legacy edit-key attestation, and open links let any link holder write. Signed presence updates with a mismatched identity or an invalid signature are rejected. Proofreading now parses content with an inert DOMParser before passing it to ProseMirror, where it previously used live innerHTML. This closes one injection path but does not show that the app is free of XSS. A smaller merged change, [#80](https://github.com/formstr-hq/nostr-docs/pull/80), adds a delete option to document cards.

Pull request [#88](https://github.com/formstr-hq/nostr-docs/pull/88), which builds on earlier work, replaces the Mammoth converter with a native OOXML adapter for DOCX files. Users can edit basic text, tables, images and page settings. Unsupported structures and the original package parts pass through unchanged. Complex and vertically merged tables, fields, footnotes and tracked changes cannot be edited. Header and footer edits are not written back, and edited runs keep only some of their formatting. Open proposal [#86](https://github.com/formstr-hq/nostr-docs/pull/86) links encrypted comments to their parents so replies can nest at any depth, and it adds tests for persisted replies. Because the parent relation sits inside the ciphertext, older clients show replies as separate comments. Resolution and counts apply at the root comment, and comment metadata is not fully private.

### Vector {#vectorprivacy-vector}

Vector, a private messaging app that hosts [Concord](/en/topics/concord-protocol/) communities, hardened locked channels in source. Locked channels rely on scoped writer certificates, quieted read addresses and sealed history from before the lock. Commits [ff610ae](https://github.com/VectorPrivacy/Vector/commit/ff610aedc460f7f1168734522d3717ba633f47ed) and [6027095](https://github.com/VectorPrivacy/Vector/commit/60270953e114fa2c2a5c11b278c764638566423e) preserve the hash, ID and read epoch of sealed content and reseal history over an initial 10-minute window. They also reject backdated reactor or call authority and mark missing history pages as incomplete. The lock applies only to new readers. Other source work adds an Add Reactions permission, guided community creation and raid detection. Raid detection examines the densest 15-minute burst of activity and distinguishes established participants from newcomers. Composer, edit-history and media handling also improved.

Merged [#101](https://github.com/VectorPrivacy/Vector/pull/101) adds a 60 ms playback buffer. Open, experimental [#102](https://github.com/VectorPrivacy/Vector/pull/102) builds a GPUI client meant to match the existing Tauri app. It has no release, and neither parity nor performance has been demonstrated.

### Wisp {#barrydeen-wisp}

Wisp, a Nostr client, merged media and payment-verification changes this week without a new release. [#688](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/688) adds [BUD-10](/en/topics/bud-10/) `blossom:` URIs: Wisp reads the file hash and server hints, tries the hinted servers and then the user's configured servers, and checks the downloaded bytes against the hash before it uses the media URL. That check covers only Wisp's own fetch, not a later independent fetch of the same URL. [#697](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/697) also verifies any supplied `sha256` against the fetched file. [BUD-05](/en/topics/bud-05/) transformed media can omit that value.

For zaps, [#687](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/687) confirms that a BOLT11 invoice commits to the expected amount and description. It also ties receipts more closely to the identity and context of the original zap request. Reading changes add article previews in feeds and profiles ([#696](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/696)), article sharing ([#692](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/692)), full note text in opened threads ([#695](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/695)), a wake lock during inline video ([#669](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/669)) and stable image heights ([#691](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/691)). Wisp now deletes superseded Drive backups and shows their status ([#689](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/689)), and it decodes entities in a single pass ([#698](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/698)).

### White Noise {#marmot-protocol-whitenoise}

White Noise, the encrypted group messenger built on the [Marmot](/en/topics/marmot/) protocol, merged work across its native apps. On Android, [#3835](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-android/pull/3835) shows the chat list and conversation side by side on screens 840dp and wider. Drafts and scroll position survive window resizes, though the selected chat is not yet highlighted. [#3839](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-android/pull/3839) opens PDF and HTML attachments inside the app. PDFs render in an isolated media process, and HTML is rebuilt from an allowlist with JavaScript and network access disabled. PDF search and text selection are not available. On iOS, [#1338](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-ios/pull/1338) accepts video, audio and documents up to 512 MiB and images up to 50 MB. Attachments over 50 MB are not saved as drafts, and backgrounding the app or leaving the chat may stop an upload. A large-file check on a physical device is still pending. [#1337](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-ios/pull/1337) adds hold-to-dictate on iOS 26 and later and sends the transcribed text on release. It uses Apple's on-device speech recognition and neither stores nor sends the audio.

On macOS, [#1031](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-mac/pull/1031) adds Save As and Show in Finder for each download. The author notes that testing in the sandboxed running app is incomplete. [#990](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-mac/pull/990) adds multi-select with bulk archive, mute and leave. Before connecting to a [NIP-46](/en/topics/nip-46/) remote signer, the Linux client [now shows](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-linux/commit/9b99d915d8488b5cd221bd6999f2f05308976d10) the exact permissions for review. It offers the pairing link only once its listener is ready and restores the vault session. Android [#3825](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-android/pull/3825), iOS [#1348](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-ios/pull/1348) and macOS [#1045](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/whitenoise-mac/pull/1045) adopt MarmotKit 0.17.0. If some invitees cannot be added, the group is still created, everyone else is invited, and the app lists the skipped users. On macOS the update migrates the account schema from version 201 to 207, and this migration cannot be reversed. A known Android problem with receiving messages after a deletion predates these changes and is still unresolved.

### ZEUS {#zeusln-zeus}

ZEUS, a Lightning wallet that can serve [Nostr Wallet Connect](/en/topics/nip-47/) ([NWC](/en/topics/nip-47/)) requests, released stable [v13.2.5](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/releases/tag/v13.2.5) on 16 November after [v13.2.5-rc1](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/releases/tag/v13.2.5-rc1). The release stores a claim for each NWC request event ID per connection and rejects repeated deliveries before they reach a handler ([#4410](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/4410)). It also adds `payment_sent` and `payment_received` notifications from [NIP-47](/en/topics/nip-47/), the wallet RPC protocol behind NWC ([#4347](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/4347)). The [v13.2.4 to v13.2.5 comparison](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/compare/v13.2.4...v13.2.5) also includes pending-invoice reconciliation and fixes to the NWC relay and initialization lifecycle.

Related NWC work merged on `master` in the same period. This issue has not confirmed which of it is in [v13.2.5](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/releases/tag/v13.2.5). `list_transactions` reconciles pending `make_invoice` activities and lists unpaid or expired invoices according to its flags ([#5610](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5610)), and pending-payment refresh uses targeted `lookupPayment` calls on backends that support them ([#4495](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/4495)). Overlapping service initializations join one in-flight operation ([#5638](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5638)). A superseded subscription can no longer become active, and concurrent subscribes on one connection join ([#5646](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5646)). A reset closes relay services after a bounded grace period for handlers still running ([#5639](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5639)). Wallet error replies keep their failure text and stay distinct from SDK transport errors ([#5758](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5758)). New custom NWC relays must use `wss`, except onion endpoints, which may use `ws` ([#5323](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5323)).

Two fixes merged after the release. [NIP-05](/en/topics/nip-05/) lookups, which map human-readable names to keys, now reject redirects ([#5371](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5371)), and an incomplete payment with an all-zero preimage no longer counts as proof of NWC settlement ([#5166](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5166)). Two draft proposals remain open. One would let users release an old pending `pay_invoice` budget hold while settlement reconciliation continues ([#5768](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5768)). The other would store the timestamp of the newest accepted request and reject older events outside a replay window ([#5726](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5726)).

### Amber {#greenart7c3-amber}

Amber, an Android signer that keeps Nostr keys for other apps, published the [7.0.0-pre4](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/releases/tag/v7.0.0-pre4) and [7.0.0-pre5](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/releases/tag/v7.0.0-pre5) prereleases. Stable 7.0.0 has not been released, and these prereleases have not had a security audit. Pre4 adds an [Amber CLI](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/commit/9ca0670db0d65dae5bc7d215cfae6c9e504452ca) for NIP-46 signing from scripts and agents, packaged for Linux, macOS and Windows. Pre5 changes CLI pairing, status reporting and secret-store handling ([#557](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/557)). The CLI subscribes before it shows a pairing URI, and `login -` reads a bunker URI from standard input. While offline or pending, `status` returns a real failure code along with `ok:false`. The CLI also keeps using whichever secret-store backend was selected.

On Android, [#565](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/565) adds `nip44v3_encrypt`, `nip44v3_decrypt` and `sign_psbt` to rate limiting. [NIP-55](/en/topics/nip-55/) is the interface Android apps use to reach a signer, and the change limits floods of approval screens requested through it while leaving cryptography unchanged. [#561](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/561) publishes the error reply for an unverified NIP-46 request once, while verified replies keep their retry behavior. Upgrade fixes restore accounts from legacy storage ([#549](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/549)), including installs from [3.3.0 or older](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/commit/71672565703537a306c03166d49bf2162af64849), and keep the upgrade and account-discovery paths intact for offline-flavor application IDs ([#550](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/550)). Smaller commits send an Amber [User-Agent](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/commit/0b0383da550dac358eef71dbbfe0a6b16d0eb3e9) from every HTTP client and [pass context to the sensitive clipboard](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/commit/313701e2a85499f7f86e46f63bfec4d160e3242d). Another commit will [make the key-store marker private and deduplicate forwarded ephemeral events](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/commit/a015f940dfaa7469bdf3c9bc4ab10478cf105e29).

### Applesauce {#hzrd149-applesauce}

Applesauce is a set of TypeScript libraries for building Nostr clients. [applesauce-extra@7.2.0](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/releases/tag/applesauce-extra%407.2.0) bounds attachment downloads. A file can be no larger than its declared size, plus the 16-byte AES-GCM authentication tag for encrypted files, and never larger than the configured maximum, which defaults to 100 MiB. A file without a valid declared size is held to that maximum. The library rejects oversized attachments before fetching them and can fall back to a mirror ([#111](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/111)). If a response declares a content length above the limit, its [body is cancelled](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/b7291eef93535cbb64db0e34283aa335873a68b7). Script-capable MIME types are returned as `application/octet-stream` unless an app explicitly allows them.

Other packages tighten event and connection handling. [applesauce-core@7.1.2](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/releases/tag/applesauce-core%407.1.2) runs an event store's configured verification on deletion events [before processing them](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/4de3bd3ebdbf5a32ea48082a44d9d2ce8a434836). [applesauce-wallet@7.1.1](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/releases/tag/applesauce-wallet%407.1.1) [filters deletion reads by the wallet owner](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/7f6619b39c69ea357b95a01446e5893e04b44c0c). [applesauce-relay@7.1.1](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/releases/tag/applesauce-relay%407.1.1) treats socket connection errors as [retryable when reconnect is enabled](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/7fffbfbf342b58fe9035c32db898b40e501cf904). Separately, merged [#119](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/119) makes Kehto DM decryption and napplet relay hints opt-in, and merged [#109](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/109) lets inbox processing continue past malformed cache rows. The available release notes do not show that either change has shipped. Merged [#121](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/121) updates the ratchet dependency.

### Hashtree {#mmalmi-hashtree}

Hashtree is a content-addressed storage system that publishes data over Nostr. Released [CLI 0.4.5](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/releases/tag/v0.4.5) and git helper 0.4.2 add name search through the social graph, with explicit publisher selection. They also add a login link shown only in the terminal and an option to publish release trees with [32-byte keys](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/fa030a54fe08c8bac234824b354e276ad29e5b0c) that older gateways can read. Suite-prefixed 33-byte keys remain the default.

TypeScript runtimes [0.5.42](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/releases/tag/hashtree-ts-runtime-v0.5.42) through [0.5.48](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/releases/tag/hashtree-ts-runtime-v0.5.48) change how browser apps read and index data. File reads keep byte order while running at most 16 concurrent store reads with up to 8 MiB of [read-ahead](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/99c495a93241f61efa85a6c12f13f30fbf0a53b5). Concurrent callers asking for the same hash [share one read](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/792aec175ee9a3b99907de39036bfc0469ec7f23). For followed-by-friends queries, the worker returns [the viewer's follows who follow the profile](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/2fb437cdf5187d8092c0e72ac7a29527d93fb9cb), and it [recomputes follow distances](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/901a95de5d0881eb833960d1f8b05902041fe458) after follow lists have been quiet for one second. Dexie 0.1.15 keeps a [transactional byte count](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/82e2ecc057e01ea9fb1614c918b47780502bb7a6) with size and access indexes, so quota and eviction checks do not load payloads. Runtime 0.5.47 adds opt-in [browser indexes for signed relay events](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/6d739cecc4b5d8e707b358e76d4230a9d85482cd), which keep older history queryable and apply deletions and replacements across stores. The publication outbox stays durable. In 0.5.48 the worker reads [signed graph snapshots](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/07f348a06750f40877fcd234ff4e6f60b7615e62) and verifies their content hash before keeping preferences for each account.

Source written after CLI 0.4.5 lets `htree get` and `cat` [open link-visible encrypted trees](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/e137c0d8850557e248eb5730e5644091a15e856e), and it removes the link secret before any discovery or HTTP request. LMDB stores [refuse lock files](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/8e9f6891fbd08f766eef263c63cf54d50d0f1bca) created under a different C library, and relay connections [race IPv6 and IPv4](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/ae95ad320c71e76e86263379a6e4ec8c32c1589f) through the WebSocket handshake. Private local HTTP routes now [reject proxied requests](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/668bb7475456ba65b02e3e7c7a490ff9100e744d) that carry forwarding markers. This only works if the proxy adds such a marker.

Paid [Cashu](/en/topics/cashu/) blob retrieval also exists only in source and is off unless configured. A configured client can move to the [paid cost tier](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/8afdf32b4b76b9d242b16678e2e28f33348626a5) once all free sources have finished without the blob, and since the [defaults change](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/d572c47ef0922583a9fa688a5bf9dcc2daf8cdf1) that includes sources that failed or timed out. The buyer [saves the payment intent and token](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/06c3794343854fb62cbb4869ca89542bd0c49166) so a payment can be recovered by its stable wallet payment ID, and it records provider shortfalls. This does not make payment and delivery atomic, and users must still trust the mint and the provider. Providers serve `hashtree.paid-blob/1` on [FIPS](/en/topics/fips/)[ port 39020](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/5cd03226693c9cc405571a384d65bcfa7928a7c7). They can also enable a custom [`POST /paid-blob`](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/92deb9cc4a7abb230f30ba165506f926233fd0f2) HTTP endpoint, which accepts requests of up to 64 KiB with sixteen concurrent slots and is not generic HTTP 402 payment. Adding an explicit paid section to the configuration turns on those [defaults](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/d572c47ef0922583a9fa688a5bf9dcc2daf8cdf1), including a 1000-sat lifetime spending limit for the buying client. Provider ledgers [write credits to disk](https://github.com/mmalmi/hashtree/commit/2872632017fbe75b7e222a04992784598888bb0b) before replying, but charges can take up to one second to be written.

### Marmot Development Kit (MDK) {#marmot-protocol-mdk}

MDK is the toolkit for building Marmot apps, which run end-to-end encrypted [MLS](/en/topics/mls/) group chats over Nostr. Stable [MDK 0.16](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.16.0), released on 12 November, brings [shared persistent NIP-46 signing](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3303) to Swift, Kotlin and C. Users must approve the exact permissions requested, and sessions resume after a restart without host callbacks. Its [OpenMLS update](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3360) includes fixes that the release's security notes describe for two bugs: nested extensions that could abort the process, and rejected private messages that could advance a sender ratchet. Existing groups still interoperate, and Hermes attachment staging and gateway files are now private.

Stable [MDK 0.17](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.17.0) followed on 15 November. It [creates groups with and invites the recipients who are available](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3375) and returns an ordered result for each member. If no recipient is available, MDK creates neither a group nor a commit. Agent-control operations still succeed or fail as a whole. [Published read snapshots](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3421) let account and group reads skip the queue behind sends and catch-up, although hydrating a group for the first time can still wait. [The release notes](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/blob/v0.17.0/docs/release/0.17.0.md) also list four concurrent attachment downloads, which are prioritized by visibility and pause when disk space is low. Missing-history notices appear only with evidence, and failed sends can be discarded locally. Polls, stickers and media gain reply context, and sticker packs get offline share links. Valid bare event references now parse, and security dependencies are updated.

Hosts upgrading to 0.17 must ship one matching set of bindings, native libraries and headers. Its account database migration from version 201 to 207 cannot be reversed. Upload of whole-file v4 audits is removed, so hosts use v5 and must initialize its source on every launch. The MDK, MarmotKit and C tags are stable, while the WN Agent v0.16 and v0.17 tags are still marked as prereleases ([0.17 release](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.17.0)).

Several changes merged after the stable releases. [Commit recovery](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3434) reads the exact pending event back after ten minutes, and [own echoes](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3430) can also confirm it. [Pending and stuck status](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3458) shows which changes are blocked, and [durable not-delivered outcomes](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3456) remain available after the originating call returns. MDK abandons a commit only when it knows the commit reached no relay; a commit that may have been exposed stays pending. [Queued sends](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3467) keep their prepared event across retries and restarts, although sends stored by older versions can still duplicate once. When host-safety checks refuse every relay, MDK returns a typed `NoUsableRelay` error ([#3515](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3515)).

[Merged device build changes](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3476) remove compile-time flags so every build can support device linking, rosters and backups. At the end of the week the runtime default was still off, and [turning it on by default](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3519) is an open pull request. These device groups are separate from the already released group membership across a user's devices. Related merged fixes restore KeyPackage rotation and avoid both idle writer transactions and empty-database creation. Another merged fix, with migration 0211, will [remember slots that were linked and then removed](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3508) after the roster removal record expires.

For messages, [editable ](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3435)[NIP-22](/en/topics/nip-22/)[ replies](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3435) recognize kind 1111 with a numeric lowercase `k` tag and keep images and author edits. [Large draft attachments](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3436) are staged as encrypted files. Native hosts can identify tombstones from a losing branch through [raw-message invalidation](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3463) and remove a single reaction through [exact-emoji removal](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3507). The 0.17 known-issues list still includes the loss of photos from old epochs. Sticker and emoji [reads](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3472) combine configured and account relays, verify copies before deduplicating them and link to the relays that served them, while publication still goes only to account relays. [Failed refreshes keep installed images](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3459), and [revisions are deduplicated before the 60-set limit applies](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3494).

When an imported relay list gives no clear answer, [onboarding repair](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3445) offers edits and default additions with explicit risk and freshness checks. [Removal advice](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3453) names relays shown to be unusable for their role. [Most idle jobs](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3258) now wake per account and run on deadlines. Session maintenance still ticks every 15 seconds, [due-time scheduling](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3361) remains open, and battery impact has not been measured.

Welcome delivery changed in two places. [Publication eligibility](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3501) pauses queued attempts when their native branch loses. [Welcome deduplication](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3520) marks content as processed only after the user completes a join or explicitly declines, so a refused wrapper no longer blocks a later honest delivery of the same bytes. Markers already set the old way remain until the ring evicts them or a fresh invite arrives. A [fix for OpenClaw tool registration and Doctor](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3518) repairs registration and upgrade failures in the Marmot plugin and is intended for 0.18.

Two proposals remain open. [Terminal reply context](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3437) would pass the replied-to snapshot as untrusted prompt data across retries, leaving command and working-directory routing unchanged. [Attachment-removal cursors](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3495) would give native hosts durable, paginated cache eviction in pages of 4,096 records, with explicit reset and reconciliation, but would not let hosts read bodies from historical records. The 0.18 nightlies from 17 November are pinned to `a2d3486acc7d`. They include [#3476](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3476), [#3467](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3467) and [#3435](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3435) and exclude [#3508](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3508), [#3518](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3518) and [#3520](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/3520). These are test builds, not a stable 0.18 release.

Iris Chat, an end-to-end encrypted messenger built on the nostr-double-ratchet library, released v2026.11.13.4. This version sends each group message as one signed sender-key ciphertext and lets clients read groups anonymously. [v2026.11.13.6](https://github.com/irislib/iris-chat-rs/releases/tag/v2026.11.13.6) sends repair requests for missing keys to the known sender device ([comparison](https://github.com/irislib/iris-chat-rs/compare/v2026.11.13...v2026.11.13.6.diff)). In the library, Rust and TypeScript code [no longer names the recipient device](https://github.com/irislib/nostr-double-ratchet/commit/d7fc6003953b9168291e4a38c2e7c22a8b535db5) on new kind 1060 messages. Sender authors learned through private key distribution are [exposed without account or device identifiers](https://github.com/irislib/nostr-double-ratchet/commit/ddb226bc51c4d2ef010356f056f4b31ef8d2bfb5), so host apps can subscribe to them anonymously. Group controls and repair still create traffic for each device. Both peers must update for new shared group traffic, and older wrapped history stays readable. According to the README, keeping historical chain seeds for recovery costs some forward secrecy. It also warns that removals must reach senders and that anonymous reads can still be linked by IP address and timing. No new library release or external audit has been published.

### Nostria {#nostria-app-nostria}

Nostria, a Nostr client, has two open pull requests, one for mute-list editing and one for browser-extension signing. With [#649](https://github.com/nostria-app/nostria/pull/649), edits to kind 10000 mute lists keep both public tags and encrypted private NIP-51 entries. ## Protocol and spec work

### NIP-73 adds OpenStreetMap object references {#nostr-protocol-nips-2224}

[NIP-73](/en/topics/nip-73/) defines identifiers that Nostr events use to reference external content. Compass covered [#2224](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2224) as an open proposal in February, and it was merged on 16 November. Events can now carry `i` tags of the form `osm:node:<id>`, `osm:way:<id>` or `osm:relation:<id>`, with `osm` as the `k` value. Because the object type is part of the identity, a node and a way with the same number are different objects. Comments can point to a specific map object and no longer have to rely on a geohash area. A reference says nothing about who owns the object, and no shipped client support has been established.

### Proposal for discovering relay onion addresses {#nostr-protocol-nips-2550}

Open proposal [#2550](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2550), a draft [NIP-0D](/en/topics/nip-0d/), lets a relay advertise an onion address for clients that cannot reach its clearnet host from a Tor exit. The relay publishes the address in an `Onion-Location` header over TLS or in a `_onion.<relay host>` DNS TXT record. Clients should prefer the header and keep the original path, map `http(s)` to `ws(s)` and connect only to the onion target, with clearnet fallback allowed as a MAY. DNSSEC or a trusted resolver is advised, since a TXT lookup made outside Tor reveals interest in the relay. AUTH may use either identity, and clients treat the advertised pair as one relay. The author reports a working relay, but general rollout is not established.

### Open proposal resolves nsites through Namecoin {#nostr-protocol-nips-2540}

Open proposal [NIPs #2540](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2540) would let a Namecoin `nostr.nsite` record point to a static site published under [NIP-5A](/en/topics/nip-5a/), using NIP-19 identifiers:

- `npub` and `nprofile` resolve to the current root manifest.
- `naddr` resolves to the current root or to a named site coordinate.
- `note` and `nevent` pin one exact manifest.

A [direct pointer](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2540) takes precedence over legacy records, and resolution stops if the lookup fails. Manifests are ordered by [NIP-01](/en/topics/nip-01/)'s event ordering and replacement rules, and files are checked against their Blossom SHA-256 hashes. The proposal separates verification against the blockchain itself from trust in an indexer. A gateway pull request cited in the proposal is not a confirmed release and does not indicate wide support.

### Open proposal defines peer-to-peer orders for non-bitcoin assets {#nostr-protocol-nips-2541}

Open proposal [NIPs #2541](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2541) would add kind 35353 for peer-to-peer orders and trades in assets other than bitcoin, with a `z` tag marking each event as an `order` or a `trade`. It reuses [NIP-69](/en/topics/nip-69/) vocabulary, but amounts use main-asset units; NIP-69 uses satoshis.

BTC, LBTC, RBTC and custodial BTC are excluded, while bitcoin-pegged tokens on other chains are allowed. The proposal separates token contracts from balances held on a platform and supports fixed prices and orders that can be taken more than once. Trade statements report the executed price and volume, which can differ from the original asking values. A signature identifies the author; it does not prove settlement or a complete trade record and cannot rule out wash trading. An earlier revision lists QvaPay as an implementation, but interoperability has not been checked ([#2541](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2541)).

### Open proposal adds L402 authentication for paid relays {#nostr-protocol-nips-2543}

[NIPs PR #2543](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2543) proposes [L402](/en/topics/l402/) authentication for paid relays over WebSocket. The relay sends a challenge containing a macaroon and a BOLT11 or BOLT12 invoice. The client pays over Lightning and returns the macaroon with the payment preimage. The relay then answers with a separate `L402-OK` message. Access goes to whoever holds the credential, so the relay needs no pubkey whitelist. Macaroon caveats limit which actions are allowed, and the relay rejects any caveat it does not recognize. Credentials can be checked without per-user state, but usage budgets, concurrency and rate limits still need counters shared for each credential. Only the issuing relay, or a sibling relay sharing its secret, can verify a credential, so it cannot be used anonymously across relays. The proposal is open, and no implementation has been identified.

### NIP-BE rewrite proposes Noise-secured Bluetooth sessions {#nostr-protocol-nips-2544}

Open pull request [#2544](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2544) rewrites [NIP-BE](/en/topics/nip-be/), which carries Nostr over Bluetooth Low Energy. Each session would use a fresh [Noise](/en/topics/noise/) XX key. Advertisements would show only the service, and users could optionally compare a six-digit code. Both sides would authenticate through a modified NIP-42 relay-authentication exchange. Basic NIP-01 relay messages and NIP-77 Negentropy sync messages would flow in both directions at once. Devices recognize each other per session and do not broadcast a permanent identity, though the privacy design has not been audited. Blob transfers can request byte ranges with optional Bao and BLAKE3 proofs. An optional L2CAP channel shares the Noise session and falls back to GATT. iOS overflow advertising works only if Android devices support discovering it. The pull request also proposes removing NIP-BE's unrecommended label, which has not been approved. The author plans to use the design in next-dipity, but it has not been deployed, tested or benchmarked there.

### AI Agent Keys proposal stores provider credentials in an encrypted list {#nostr-protocol-nips-2545}

Open proposal [#2545](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2545), a draft [NIP-FA](/en/topics/nip-fa-ai-agent-keys/), would let users carry AI provider configuration between clients. It defines a replaceable kind 12000 event holding an ordered list of provider keys and endpoints, NIP-44 encrypted to the author's own key. Public key tags are ignored, each key may be used only with its own URL, and endpoints must use HTTPS unless they are loopback or keyless. Anyone holding the account key, or able to perform NIP-44 decryption for it, can spend against those credentials, subject only to provider-side limits. Configuration becomes portable without an agent framework being defined, and no deployment or interoperability between providers has been verified.

### Scheduled Tasks proposed as NIP-FB {#nostr-protocol-nips-2546}

Open [#2546](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2546) proposes scheduled tasks that run on the user's own devices. Each task is a kind 34817 event with a POSIX cron schedule and an IANA timezone. Task state, claims and completions are private. Each device opts in to running tasks and waits a random delay before claiming one. Devices read from and write to a majority of the user's NIP-65 write relays, then wait 20 seconds for a claim to settle. An optional public runner named in a `p` tag can decrypt and run a task, signs as itself and must check that the requester is authorized. The draft also has rules for daylight saving time and missed runs. Quartz includes some of the building blocks, but no complete scheduler has been shown.

### Proposed [NIP-FC](/en/topics/nip-fc/) publishes Maven repositories through Nostr and Blossom {#nostr-protocol-nips-2547}

An [open proposal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2547) describes how to publish JVM, Kotlin and Android artifacts as Blossom files. Each release has a canonical SHA-256 manifest and signed kind 3071 release events, plus addressable kind 33071 snapshots. A bridge serves standard Maven paths. A NIP-05 identifier names the publisher and pins its keys. NIP-65 relay lists and server discovery locate the files. Consumers record the publisher keys and the manifest hash. If a key is compromised, the earliest timestamp does not prove immutability, so continuity depends on local pins. A Gradle integration was not established in the reviewed evidence, and the proposal does not replace Maven Central.

### Proposed [NIP-1B](/en/topics/nip-1b/) shares network abuse reports {#nostr-protocol-nips-2548}

An [open proposal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2548) lets operators of any service publish reports of attacks and probes as addressable kind 31984 events. A report can be corrected by replacing it. Single-letter tags make /24 IPv4 ranges, /64 IPv6 ranges, ASNs, apps, user agents and operations searchable, and [NIP-32](/en/topics/nip-32/) labels add classification. The text warns that a Tor or VPN exit address does not identify an attacker and discourages harmful detail. A reporter's signature shows who made the allegation, not that it is true. The author cites about 155 reports covering 9,400 sources on a read-only relay. Independent verification of those figures was not established in the reviewed evidence, and the data should not be used as a trustworthy automatic blocklist.

### Scoped Data Grants separates issuer and reader rules {#jafairweather-nostr-scoped-data-grants}

[Scoped Data Grants](/en/topics/scoped-data-grants/) is a proposal for signed capability grants, using kinds 440 and 441, that give access to scoped data. Its [latest revision](https://github.com/JAFairweather/nostr-scoped-data-grants/commit/6ea52227844909e45657d1bc5442b27dbafb0592) defines the grant's shape for issuers separately from the rules readers apply. It adds signed refusal fixtures and a local demo of issuing and narrowing grants. It is not an assigned official NIP.

### Nostr Archive Standards revise archive declarations to v2 {#nostr-archive-standards}

[Nostr Archive Standards](/en/topics/nostr-archive-standards/) (NAS), a set of specifications for verifiable Parquet archives of Nostr events, moved its archive declarations and its extracted-data collections and catalogs to version 2 ([standards commit](https://gitworkshop.dev/npub1manlnflyzyjhgh970t8mmngrdytcp3jrmaa66u846ggg7t20cgqqvyn9tn/relay.ngit.dev/nas?commit=27c573c52bb8f84159c1d31539c2aa75c2667899)). The reference [data extractor](https://gitworkshop.dev/npub1manlnflyzyjhgh970t8mmngrdytcp3jrmaa66u846ggg7t20cgqqvyn9tn/relay.ngit.dev/nas-data-extractor?commit=80204569ebeec233b282abfb3ef851b026f1cc0b) implements the revised declarations and v2 identifiers, and it compacts conflicting designations. It is a local file library and does not run as a relay service. NAS is not an accepted NIP.

### Blossom proposes scoped delegation to another signing key {#hzrd149-blossom-118}

Blossom, a protocol for media servers that store files by hash and authorize requests with Nostr events, has an open proposal for [BUD-11](/en/topics/bud-11/)[ delegation](https://github.com/hzrd149/blossom/pull/118). The owner signs a kind 24244 token that names one actor, explicit verbs, required server scopes and an expiry. The actor embeds this token in its own kind 24242 authorization. Server and hash scopes intersect, and storage ownership stays with the delegator. A revocation blob under the owner's direct control disables the delegation, and deleting that marker turns it back on. Tokens must not be published to relays. Mailstr attachments motivated the proposal, and no server or client conformance has been established.

### Blossom mirroring gains SSRF guidance {#hzrd149-blossom-4c467fe8d483}

Blossom, the protocol for storing Nostr media blobs on HTTP servers, defines a mirror operation in [BUD-04](/en/topics/bud-04/) in which a server fetches a blob from a client-supplied URL. A [merged specification change](https://github.com/hzrd149/blossom/commit/4c467fe8d483ad0c3dda784707dfee4436793650) recommends that servers accept only HTTP and HTTPS mirror URLs and block internal, loopback, private and link-local IPv4 and IPv6 destinations. Servers should apply those checks to the IP address they connect to after DNS resolution and again on every redirect; an upload authorization does not replace this protection. This is normative SHOULD guidance that each server still has to implement, and the change establishes no server fix.

### Marmot counts an exact relay read-back as a publish acknowledgement {#marmot-protocol-marmot-504}

The Marmot group-messaging specification merged [#504](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/504) for clients that lose a relay's `OK` reply. A client can now treat a publish as acknowledged when a relay returns the exact event, with a valid ID and signature, in response to a REQ; results on an existing subscription also count. An empty REQ result proves neither publication nor failure. Clients SHOULD read back their own kind 445 events under the ephemeral key without authenticating as their account, so the read-back cannot link the two. An acknowledgement shows that a relay stored the event, not that the group agrees on its state. The wire format does not change.

### Marmot completes relay targets an abandoned publish never reached {#marmot-protocol-marmot-505}

Marmot, a protocol for MLS-based group messaging over Nostr, tracks publish obligations per relay. Under durability case 1, [a merged change](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/505) completes the relay targets that an abandoned publish obligation recorded as never sent. A rejecting `OK` does not count as never sent, because the relay received the event. This closes a case where obligations could stay stranded, and nothing changes on the wire.

### Marmot requires extended-length NIP-44 encryption for large messages {#marmot-protocol-marmot-508}

The Marmot protocol for MLS group messaging over Nostr merged [PR #508](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/508) into its specification. Marmot clients and the NIP-44 signers they use must encrypt and decrypt the extended-length version 2 format for plaintexts of 65,536 bytes or more. Notification servers must be able to decrypt it. Group and inbox relays should accept serialized events of at least 2 MiB. Under these limits, a Welcome for one invitee could cover roughly 1,900 members, but only when the group's tree has blank parent nodes, so this is not a tested limit for every group. The change adds no version negotiation, and local resource limits still apply.

### Marmot proposes per-message expiry and burn-after-read {#marmot-protocol-marmot-506}

Marmot, a protocol for MLS-encrypted group messaging over Nostr, has an [open draft proposal](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/506) for disappearing messages. For authenticated kind 9 app messages, a sender can set an absolute expiration cap that shortens the group's retention for the source epoch. A versioned `marmot-expiry` tag adds a timer on the device that starts when the message is read, plus a required cap for messages that are never opened. Durations range from one second to four weeks. The deadline is saved before the content is shown and survives replay and restart. Burned content is left out of supporting history and backups. Older clients may ignore the tag, and recipients can still copy content, so deletion relies on cooperating clients and cannot be enforced. No MDK implementation or capability negotiation has been established.

### NAP-INTENT reconciles handler recommendations and catalog identities {#napplet-naps-106}

The napplet specifications merged [#106](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/106) into NAP-INTENT, the draft for app-to-host invocation and handler selection, on `master`. It merges duplicate `IntentInvokeOptions` schemas and turns a bare `naddr` recommendation fragment into a `handlerHint`, kept separate from the convention's identity and payload. When an app names a handler explicitly, it uses an opaque catalog identity that is distinct from a recommendation coordinate. Malformed or duplicate hints are rejected. Neither an operation nor a capability is added, and there is no new precedence rule. The existing web projection is aligned with these rules.

### NAP-LLM adds optional consent for multi-step sessions {#napplet-naps-143}

[NAP-LLM](/en/topics/nap-llm/), a napplet proposal that lets an app ask its host runtime for language-model generations, merged [PR #143](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/143) as development on `nap-llm`, not yet integrated into `master`. A runtime may offer one visible, revocable grant that covers several generations on one handle while the tool set stays the same. The grant must disclose that later prompts, images and tool results will not be reviewed one by one. It must also state limits on duration, count and charges. Authorizing each generation remains the default, and users can decline a session and approve generations one at a time. Revoking the grant cancels work in progress, and generations with an uncertain outcome are not retried automatically. No runtime implements it yet.

### [NAP-WEBRTC](/en/topics/nap-webrtc/) adds data channel semantics {#napplet-naps-145}

Merged [#145](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/145) is development on `nap-webrtc`, not yet integrated into `master`. Its target, the [base proposal][nap-webrtc-base], is still open. Napplets can request ordering and retransmission settings, and the shell reports the settings in effect. A separate `sendBytes` call uses base64 on the wire, and the change also adds advertised payload limits and advisory stats. The peer's identity must be the signaling key the shell has authenticated, and unauthenticated payloads are not delivered. The proposal says no implementation exists yet.

### [NAP-NEIGHBOR](/en/topics/nap-neighbor/) proposes peer-carried neighborhoods for napplets {#napplet-naps-146}

Open draft proposal [NAP-NEIGHBOR](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/146) gives each napplet opaque neighborhood addresses for relay, outbox, resource and upload use, carried over the user's own peers. Each user has a separate view of their own peers. The host manages the neighborhood and keeps each napplet's addresses separate. Optionally, the host can share proximity information and napplet updates. A neighborhood has no membership list and no privacy boundary, and peers can forward ordinary Nostr events to others. Specs that consume these addresses would need new schemes and upload destinations. No transport implementation exists, and neither delivery nor reach is guaranteed. A companion registry change is part of the same proposal, which replaces earlier mesh drafts that were withdrawn.

### NAP-INTENT proposes opening applets by manifest coordinate {#napplet-naps-148}

NAP-INTENT covers how applets ask the host shell to invoke other apps and how the shell picks handlers. Open [#148](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/148) lets a manifest coordinate name exactly one installed applet to open or bring into focus. It can also offer an optional verified install, which the user must confirm. Under the proposal, the shell cannot substitute a different handler, change defaults, grant permissions or deliver payloads. Payloads stay with `invoke`. The proposer links a Myco implementation.

### NAP-WEBMCP proposes permissioned tools from running napplets {#napplet-naps-150}

Open draft [#150](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/150) proposes [NAP-WEBMCP](/en/topics/nap-webmcp/), which would let running napplets register typed tools for AI agents through the host shell. Registering a tool neither authorizes an agent nor grants host permissions; the shell decides which consumers can reach each tool and within what scope. Resetting the document or context removes the tools and cancels open calls, and a call that finishes late is reported separately from the acknowledgement of its cancellation. The bridge bounds JSON schema size, checks replays and mismatched correlations, returns explicit errors on overload and does not automatically retry calls with an uncertain outcome. An optional browser projection is included. It is not a finished browser API, and agents have not been tested against it for conformance.

### Cyberspace keeps claimable assets out of public contents hints {#arkin0x-cyberspace-111}

[Cyberspace](/en/topics/cyberspace/), a specification for a virtual world built on Nostr, merged [PR #111](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/111). Public contents hints now leave out claimable Cashu tokens and NFTs unless the publisher opts in. The PR gives guidance for rewriting hints safely, and NFT hints no longer include a count. Readers should treat contents hints as unverified claims. They do not prove that an asset exists, keep anything private or establish proximity. The change is in the draft specification only and is not in any released client.

### Cyberspace avatars drop proof of work and cap content size {#arkin0x-cyberspace-109}

Cyberspace is a draft spec for a spatial world built on Nostr. Merged [#109](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/109) removes the proof-of-work requirement for avatars, which are published as replaceable kind 11333 events. Before parsing, clients must check that avatar content is at most 131072 bytes of UTF-8. They should ignore the old nonce target and show the default avatar when content is too large. A note about signer size limits is non-normative.

### Cyberspace draft adds a no-broadcast courtesy for audiences {#arkin0x-cyberspace-106}

Cyberspace, a protocol for a shared spatial world built from Nostr events, merged [#106](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/106) into its draft specification. The change defines a courtesy signal asking audiences not to rebroadcast an event; ordinary readers are unaffected. Clients can ignore the signal, the data stays public and base-chain validity does not change. No client has adopted this draft change yet.

### Cyberspace drafts a Games DECK for world discovery and game runs {#arkin0x-cyberspace-96}

Cyberspace, a specification for a shared spatial world built from Nostr events, merged [#96](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/96), its first draft Games DECK. The draft defines how games are discovered in the world and adds an entry marker. Game runs are anchored to that marker and use ephemeral events in the existing kind 20000 to 29999 range, so the draft adds no new kind. No client or production game implements the draft yet.

### Cyberspace DNA seed revisions bind their own interpretation {#arkin0x-cyberspace-99}

Cyberspace is a protocol for a shared virtual space built from Nostr events. A DNA seed's revision, identified by its content ID, now determines how validity, pricing, growth, mixing and backward interpretation are evaluated, which makes a separate seed version field unnecessary ([#99](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/99)). Implementers read a seed's rules from the revision its content ID names. This merged change applies to a draft specification, and revision 1 becomes fixed only when the document leaves Draft. The errata so far are not a released implementation, and no client deploys them.

### Cyberspace proposes signed per-world game rules {#arkin0x-cyberspace-105}

An [open Cyberspace proposal](https://github.com/arkin0x/cyberspace/pull/105) lets a world list its required and optional games-layer rules inline in its signed definition. Game clients lock that configuration for each session and reject worlds whose required rules they do not support. This sets a contract for game clients without changing base-chain validity or requiring the tutorial to be republished. The proposal is still an open draft, and no client has deployed it.

## NIP deep dive: public goals and peer-to-peer orders

Two Nostr specs let people publish requests that involve money. A [NIP-75](/en/topics/nip-75/) [zap goal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/999f9bfbf5fe00d5c2711fd24badb4e56748c9bc/75.md) announces a fundraising target that readers can zap toward, and a [NIP-69](/en/topics/nip-69/) [order](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md) advertises an offer to buy or sell bitcoin for fiat. Clients can find, render and filter both kinds of signed event, though neither kind moves funds. Goal progress comes from separate [NIP-57](/en/topics/nip-57/) zap receipts, while trades are taken, quoted and settled on the platform that published the offer. Neither spec requires the other.

### Zap goals

The [pinned NIP-75 text](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/999f9bfbf5fe00d5c2711fd24badb4e56748c9bc/75.md) defines kind 9041 as a regular event, so each goal stands alone with no replaceable address. Its content holds a human-readable description, and two tags are required: `amount` gives the target in millisatoshis, and `relays` is a single tag listing the relays that zaps to the goal are sent to and tallied from. Optional tags add:

- a `closed_at` timestamp;
- `image` and `summary` display metadata;
- an `r` or `a` tag pointing to an external URL or an addressable event;
- one or more `zap` tags that name split beneficiaries under NIP-57 Appendix G.

A [NIP-57](/en/topics/nip-57/) zap request aimed directly at a goal carries the goal's id in its `e` tag. Under the [pinned NIP-75 text](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/999f9bfbf5fe00d5c2711fd24badb4e56748c9bc/75.md), the client MUST also copy the goal's relays into the request's `relays` tag, so receipts land on the relays that the goal names for tallying. An addressable event, such as a [NIP-53](/en/topics/nip-53/) live stream, can point to a goal with a `goal` tag holding the goal id and an optional relay hint; when users zap that event, clients SHOULD put the goal id in the request's `e` tag. Relay hints help clients retrieve events without establishing trust or confirming payment.

Progress is the sum of zap receipts that reference the goal and pass [NIP-57](/en/topics/nip-57/) validation, which includes checking the bolt11 amount and confirming that the receipt came from the correct zap provider for the correct target. Each receipt should count once, and NIP-75 does not define a complete tallying algorithm. Because readers see only what their relays return, a client that reaches only some of the goal's relays may show an incomplete total; when those relays already hold every receipt, the total is complete. For `closed_at`, the [pinned NIP-75 text](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/999f9bfbf5fe00d5c2711fd24badb4e56748c9bc/75.md) says that receipts published after that timestamp SHOULD NOT count toward progress. That rule governs only what clients count: NIP-75 attaches no payment cutoff or refund to it and, unlike [NIP-40](/en/topics/nip-40/) expiration, asks relays to delete nothing.

Retrieved from nos.lol and relay.damus.io, the event below is a real goal whose content identifies it as a qiqstr QA test and asks readers to ignore it. It appears here only to illustrate the [NIP-75](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/999f9bfbf5fe00d5c2711fd24badb4e56748c9bc/75.md) format and is no request for donations.

```json
{
  "kind": 9041,
  "id": "499444115e01a11ae3663d594b7bd35f731997b8ede46a86aa9a1184c892e03a",
  "pubkey": "cfdd4a9981b235b8b585480b752fe5be0207be3dfebca0ff5326d7c465646866",
  "created_at": 1794805859,
  "tags": [
    [
      "amount",
      "1000000"
    ],
    [
      "relays",
      "wss://relay.primal.net",
      "wss://vitor.nostr1.com",
      "wss://relay.damus.io",
      "wss://nos.lol"
    ],
    [
      "emoji",
      "100_yeet",
      "https://poster.place/client/emoji/reactions/100_yeet.png"
    ]
  ],
  "content": "qiqstr QA zap goal test, please ignore  :100_yeet:",
  "sig": "1745910f6935c83a2cd1761cb7c905010659bdb6d4999bc86f9aeac0fd4fa017027bd5fa7b000b5ab3242e5963f164bfa24ecda8235e04fd92d3a5c82d172a76"
}
```

An `amount` of `1000000` millisatoshis sets a target of 1,000 sats. A client zapping this goal would copy all four URLs from the single `relays` tag into its zap request, while the `emoji` tag only tells clients how to draw the `:100_yeet:` shortcode in the content and carries no payment meaning. No `closed_at`, image, summary, link or beneficiary tag is present. Under [NIP-01](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/01.md), `id` is the SHA-256 hash of the canonical serialized event and `sig` is a BIP-340 signature over that id by `pubkey`. Both verify, showing that this key signed this exact content with the `created_at` value 1794805859; whether anyone has zapped the goal is outside what either field records.

[Amethyst](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst), the Nostr client for Android, creates goals. At commit [`d48764f`](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/commit/d48764fab810e5c4fb966d19cf4c7a905e38c0f7), its [goal creation view model](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/blob/d48764fab810e5c4fb966d19cf4c7a905e38c0f7/commonsUI/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/commons/ui/screen/loggedIn/home/nip75Goals/NewGoalViewModel.kt#L55) accepts a positive whole number of sats and multiplies it by 1,000 for the `amount` tag, then fills `relays` with the user's current outbox relays. Before signing and broadcasting the goal, it can add a deadline, image, summary and website. Goal progress bars and zap buttons reached its live-stream headers earlier, in [PR #2469](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/2469).

[NosTube](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube), a Nostr video client with live streams, reads goals for streams. At commit [`0467076`](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube/commit/04670761805c5c19c76844658e92e1629cae6960), it [fetches](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube/blob/04670761805c5c19c76844658e92e1629cae6960/apps/web/src/hooks/useZapGoals.ts#L64) the goal linked from a live stream or, when the stream links none, picks the host's newest open goal among up to twenty recent ones. Its [parser](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube/blob/04670761805c5c19c76844658e92e1629cae6960/packages/core/src/zap-goal.ts#L35) requires kind 9041 and an amount of at least 1,000 millisatoshis, checks that the id and pubkey are hex-shaped, accepts only HTTPS images and keeps at most ten distinct relay hints; it performs no signature check of its own. During its [progress calculation](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube/blob/04670761805c5c19c76844658e92e1629cae6960/packages/core/src/zap-goal.ts#L80), NosTube drops receipts timestamped after `closed_at` and sums the sats from receipts returned by a separate receipt validator. Its [goal bar](https://github.com/flox1an/nostube/blob/04670761805c5c19c76844658e92e1629cae6960/apps/web/src/components/live/ZapGoalBar.tsx#L15) passes the goal id and relays to the zap button and hides that button once the goal has closed or when the host has no Lightning address.

[Coracle](https://github.com/coracle-social/coracle), a web Nostr client, displays goals. At commit [`20cd3da`](https://github.com/coracle-social/coracle/commit/20cd3daa0bd438c1be68a79f72d6e06578f46e1b), its note renderer [sends kind 9041](https://github.com/coracle-social/coracle/blob/20cd3daa0bd438c1be68a79f72d6e06578f46e1b/src/app/shared/NoteContent.svelte#L105) to a [dedicated component](https://github.com/coracle-social/coracle/blob/20cd3daa0bd438c1be68a79f72d6e06578f46e1b/src/app/shared/NoteContentKind9041.svelte#L11) that shows the amount in sats, an optional deadline and the description. Goal creation and receipt totals fall outside the cited code.

Goal descriptions and images are untrusted input, as the [NIP-75 topic](/en/topics/nip-75/) notes. A client should never execute HTML from a description or present a malformed amount or timestamp as funded progress, and it should limit relay subscriptions and media downloads. Goals and their receipts are public. Because each [NIP-57](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nostr-protocol/nips/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/57.md) receipt embeds the signed zap request, a reader can connect the key that signed a request, together with the amount it named, to a particular fundraiser. Receipts are issued by the recipient's zap provider, and a request key does not prove who paid the invoice. A pseudonymous request names its signing key without establishing the payer's real identity, while an anonymous request may hide the payer altogether. Readers also see only the receipts that the queried relays return.

### Peer-to-peer orders

The [pinned NIP-69 text](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md) defines kind 38383 as an [addressable event](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/01.md#kinds), identified by author, kind and `d` tag. A platform updates an order by publishing a new event at the same coordinate, which replaces the earlier version under NIP-01 rules. An event from a different author with the same `d` value is a separate order. NIP-69 describes `d` as a unique identifier for the order without requiring a UUID format.

Besides `d`, the [spec](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md) marks these tags as required:

- `k`: `buy` or `sell`.
- `f`: an ISO 4217 currency code.
- `s`: the status, one of `pending`, `canceled`, `in-progress`, `success` or `expired`.
- `amt`: the bitcoin amount in satoshis. With a value of `0`, the satoshi amount is obtained from a public API after the taker accepts the order.
- `fa`: a fiat amount, or two values giving the minimum and maximum of a range.
- `pm`: the payment method or methods.
- `premium`: the premium percentage the maker is willing to pay.
- `network` and `layer`: the bitcoin network and the settlement layer.
- `expires_at` and `expiration`: the two time tags described below.
- `y`: the platform that created the order.
- `z`: the fixed value `order`.

[Optional tags](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md):

- `source`: a URL that can redirect to the order.
- `rating`: a maker rating, calculated however the platform chooses.
- `name`: the maker's name.
- `g`: a geohash used for face-to-face trades.
- `bond`: the amount of a security deposit.

Each time tag has its own role in the [pinned NIP-69 text](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md). After `expires_at`, a pending order's status SHOULD change to `expired`; after `expiration`, relays SHOULD delete the event under [NIP-40](/en/topics/nip-40/). Relays can deliver events late or return versions that a newer event at the same coordinate has replaced under [NIP-01 addressable-event rules](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/01.md#kinds). Before showing an order as open, an orderbook should confirm that it holds the newest `created_at` for that coordinate and that its `s` status and `expires_at` still describe an open offer.

Publishing an order advertises an offer without reserving liquidity, holding escrow, proving funds or settling a trade. Taking the offer, quoting the price, handling disputes and holding funds all stay on the [publishing platform](/en/topics/nip-69/). A valid signature identifies the broadcaster, which is often a platform key. The signature says nothing about solvency or honest trading. Similarly, a `bond` tag announces a deposit without proving that anyone paid it. Ratings from different platforms are not comparable, since [NIP-69](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/f63c00213fbe494983f2753018d829ea64724d83/69.md) leaves the calculation to each platform. Clients should present `source` as a link the user chooses to follow and never run it as code. Fiat currency, payment methods, geohash and posting times are all public, and together they can reveal a trader's habits.

[Mostro](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro), a peer-to-peer Lightning exchange that coordinates trades over Nostr, is checked here at commit [`2211a7e`](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/commit/2211a7e02bba87416a66026bc653ae1bff886b2a). It [publishes public orders](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/blob/2211a7e02bba87416a66026bc653ae1bff886b2a/src/util.rs#L475) with empty content and the order's UUID as `d`, recording the id of each event. Its `created_at` values always increase, even when a take or cancel lands in the same second as the first pending event, so the later event still replaces the pending one under NIP-01 ordering. Before publishing, its [order validation](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/blob/2211a7e02bba87416a66026bc653ae1bff886b2a/src/app/order.rs#L131) rejects ranges with only one end and checks fixed fiat amounts, non-negative sats, range limits and quote bounds.

[RoboSats](https://github.com/RoboSats/robosats), a Lightning peer-to-peer exchange run by independent coordinators, is checked at commit [`babf62e`](https://github.com/RoboSats/robosats/commit/babf62e2238f262182d0bf638496aaa53e286853). It [signs orders](https://github.com/RoboSats/robosats/blob/babf62e2238f262182d0bf638496aaa53e286853/api/nostr.py#L36) with the coordinator's key and sends them to a local strfry relay, deriving each `d` value as a UUID from an MD5 hash of the coordinator alias and the order id. Its tags cover type, currency, amount or range, payment methods, premium, source, expiration, bond, platform, network and layer, with a geohash added when coordinates exist. A published order maps to `pending` and every other internal state maps to `success`, so only two of the five defined statuses appear. RoboSats builds `pm` values by splitting the payment-method text on spaces.

[lnp2pBot](https://github.com/lnp2pBot/bot), a Telegram bot for peer-to-peer Lightning trades, is checked at commit [`6d91a79`](https://github.com/lnp2pBot/bot/commit/6d91a794d03da0789f88331c28039e95724c3fc9). Its [tag builder](https://github.com/lnp2pBot/bot/blob/6d91a794d03da0789f88331c28039e95724c3fc9/bot/modules/nostr/events.ts#L11) emits the order fields `d`, `k`, `f`, `s`, `amt`, `fa` (one amount or a range), `pm` and `premium`, along with fixed values `network=mainnet`, `layer=lightning`, `y=lnp2pbot` and `z=order`. A local `rating` combines the maker's rating, account age and review count. For `source`, the builder links to Telegram, choosing the community channel for community orders, and it takes `expiration` from its configuration.

Absent from that output are the required `expires_at` tag and the optional `bond`. Whenever a public order changes, the bot [signs and verifies](https://github.com/lnp2pBot/bot/blob/6d91a794d03da0789f88331c28039e95724c3fc9/bot/modules/nostr/events.ts#L80) an event and then [publishes it](https://github.com/lnp2pBot/bot/blob/6d91a794d03da0789f88331c28039e95724c3fc9/bot/modules/nostr/index.ts#L27) to its configured relays. Differences such as RoboSats' two-status mapping and lnp2pBot's missing `expires_at` give an orderbook spanning several platforms good reason to parse orders defensively.
