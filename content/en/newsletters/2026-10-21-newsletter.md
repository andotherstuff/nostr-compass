[Keep Android 1.3.2](https://github.com/privkeyio/keep-android/releases/tag/v1.3.2) revokes earlier bunker grants and scopes them per account. It also drops a compromised bundled CA root and makes storage fail closed. [Nostr WoT extension 0.8.14](https://github.com/nostr-wot/nostr-wot-extension/releases/tag/v0.8.14) adds experimental phone signing over WiFi or Web Bluetooth, and local keys stay unused if the phone disconnects. [Zapstore 1.1.3](https://github.com/zapstore/zapstore/releases/tag/1.1.3) carries app comments and zaps across a catalog signing key change. Catalog links carry over too. [nostr-pubsub 0.5.19](https://github.com/mmalmi/nostr-pubsub/releases/tag/nostr-pubsub-ts-v0.5.19) limits identified receiver EVENT and INV exports to explicit public authors, while IP and timing remain visible. A [merged Napplet change](https://github.com/napplet/web/pull/261) checks the event ID and signature before headless browser execution. It also checks the exact raw HTML hash first. The deep dive contrasts [NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md) ([topic](/en/topics/nip-37/)) encrypted drafts with [NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md) ([topic](/en/topics/nip-48/)) signed bridged events.

## Top Stories

### diVine

diVine is the short-video Nostr client. diVine published [stable release 1.0.25](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/releases/tag/1.0.25) on 19 October, built from its tagged source. It adds motion tracking along with masks and stabilization. Recording backups and hashtag follows are also new. Users can dismiss a chat while keeping its history, and sound reuse requests ask for creator consent. Direct message catch-up is more reliable, with page caps and a Following cursor. Legacy [NIP-04](/en/topics/nip-04/) copies now reuse events that are already signed, with bounded retries. Sender copies of reactions reach advertised inbox relays. Account-switch list isolation is still experimental. Related merged work: [account-key cleanup](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10030), [legacy DM relay recovery](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10149), [sender reaction inbox copies](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10153), [moderation message classification](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10158), [Following page continuity](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10185), [bounded legacy DM copies](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10188), [concurrent relay sends](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10190), [same-second Following cursor](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10211), [durable DM catch-up](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10212), [hashtag follows](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10213), [conversation dismissal](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10221), [sound reuse consent](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10222), [iOS recording backup](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10223), [Android recording backup](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10224), [motion tracking](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10235), [older DM pagination](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10269), [experimental account list isolation](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10289).

Release status for source changes merged after 1.0.25 is unconfirmed. [Inbox history evidence](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10295) requires a live inbox list before history is marked complete. With [explicit reaction retry](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10319), retry budgets stay available while the user's own relays are down. A [NOTICE redaction change](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10320) strips IP addresses from relay NOTICE messages, while raw CLOSED reasons are still shown as received. [Mark-as-read feedback](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10321) now reports how many messages matched and whether a batch was partial. Read state still syncs across the user's devices through self-addressed messages, and read receipts visible to the other person remain unimplemented. [Follow load coalescing](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10296) removes duplicate contact-list queries and subscriptions. When the user switches accounts, the old account's scheduled-post relay step is cancelled, while other requests, logs and repository state from that account can persist. [Rooms](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10093) remains a public test proof of concept with its release flag off.

Other merged source work adds [on-device Smart people ordering](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10172), which ranks people by cached follows, accepted one-to-one conversations and activity. Smart is the default for the user's own follow lists, with chronological order still available. Exact search matches rank first, and moving someone up takes more than an inbound follow alone. The follower cache is kept per account, with incremental refreshes at most once per 15 minutes and a full refresh weekly. When a read hits its cap before finishing, older rows are kept. Rankings and local message content stay off relays. A [private-key search guard](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10261) stops searches and lookups containing nsec1 or ncryptsec1 strings before any request is sent. Matching is case-insensitive and also catches keys with a nostr prefix or zero-width characters, and the warning omits the key itself. Because the guard matches on the prefix, incomplete or mistyped keys are caught too. Its coverage is limited to search: bare 64-character hex passes through by design, and published captions and comments go out unfiltered, so secrets can still leak through those paths. Copying or sharing a [verified Divine username](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10323) now uses its HTTPS username URL, falling back to the npub when the name is unverified or unavailable, with the cache keyed to the exact claim. This change is source-only and absent from app-store releases.

The [handle-ownership check for username search](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10369) would confirm who owns a Divine handle by asking Divine's name server. Today the app trusts the [NIP-05](/en/topics/nip-05/) claim on a profile or the search index. The proposal covers root `_@user.divine.video` handles and would put the real owner first, even when another account uses the same display name. The lookup would run in parallel with search and stop after 3 seconds. If it fails or the handle shows as free, pending or reserved, the old results would stay as they are, and blocks would still apply. The cost would be one extra request for each debounced username search. This is not general NIP-05 validation.

The [received video DM handling proposal](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/pull/10349) would download and decrypt each received video once, show a real frame from it and reuse the copy. Videos would only load automatically from people you follow or chats you have replied in. Any reply counts, even one declining the conversation. Auto-loading tells the media host when you view the chat. Videos from strangers you have never replied to would load only when you tap them. Blocking or muting a sender would stop downloading and decoding. Automatic downloads would run one at a time, with a 25 MiB and one-minute limit. They would be cancelled when the video scrolls offscreen, unless playback has started. Tap-to-load would keep the existing 100 MB limit. HTTPS redirects would be capped at five, and their bytes would count toward the total. The proposal does not hide metadata in general.

### Amber

Amber merged a set of signer hardening changes in source on 16 October. Its require-unlocked-device protection is opt-in and defaults to false. When a user enables it, new checks require an unlocked device before the cached private key is used. Background signing follows the configured device-lock policy. Other merged work covers bunker request routing, backup seed validation, consent before relay rating, secured WebDAV handling and isolation of timed permissions ([bunker routing](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/532), [opt-in device lock checks](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/534), [proxied WebDAV transport](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/535), [relay-rating consent](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/536), [backup seed restoration](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/537), [timed permission isolation](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber/pull/538)).

### Amethyst

Amethyst is the Android and desktop Nostr social client. Merged source since the 1.19.0 release hardens how the client handles untrusted input. Embed decompression now has a size limit, Nostr Wallet Connect responses are verified, addressable events sort correctly, and private zaps check their target. Cancelling work no longer corrupts group recovery. A resumed bulk export now commits its cursor only after delivery, and fixes to outbox and reconnect handling stop relay events from being lost. [NIP-34](/en/topics/nip-34/) repository creation now reaches GRASP relays and keeps unknown tags. This work landed in [account-scoped deck and group resets](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4481), [browser data clearing](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4486), [malformed-host crash prevention](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4492), [remote-signer Tor routing](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4493), [large Cyberspace objects](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4498), [signed desktop updates](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4500), [list-definition cards](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4501), [Open Markets parsing](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4506), [bulk ingest and resumable exports](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4510), [relay and event-handling repairs](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4514) and [in-flight relay retention](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4515).

On desktop, signed updates are checked against the expected author and file hash, and the GitHub fallback only links to downloads. OpenMarkets cards can now tell apart encrypted Shopstr kinds that share kind numbers. Other additions include list-definition cards, GIF support in drawings, shape-aware proof of work, separate storage for each desktop instance, and fixes for video startup on Linux. Large Cyberspace objects that are valid no longer hit an outdated geometry limit. All of this is merged source with no new release yet: see [Open Markets cards](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4516), [interrupted paged fetches](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4517), [subscription replay and outbox delivery](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4521), [relay-client API repairs](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4522), [input validation and cancellation](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4530), [additional event cards](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4534), [NIP-34 repository creation](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4541), [drawing replay GIFs](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4543), [desktop process isolation](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4562), [proof-of-work hashing](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4563) and [Linux video startup](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4567).

Relay delivery got stricter after that. [Treating ordinary OK replies as final](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4589) means that once a relay accepts or refuses an event, the client stops holding it and skips resending it on reconnect. Auth-required and rate-limited replies still get a bounded number of retries. [Counted connection leases](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4591) build on that, with callers now explicitly keeping and releasing sockets. An unanswerable auth-required refusal is final, and the client waits through at most 10 auth-required replies. Publishing on a socket that is already open starts right away. A replay race while a socket is still connecting can send an event twice, and the client ignores the duplicate OK.

[Remote-signer fixes](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4568) change several parts of the [NIP-46](/en/topics/nip-46/) flow. An auth_url challenge now waits for the user to open it from an explicit popup. Client and signer coordinate switch_relays, with a grace period for replies still arriving on the old relays. Logout sends a best-effort notice for up to 15 seconds, and local key removal goes ahead regardless. The command-line tool identifies the account through get_public_key, separate from the transport key. It fails if that lookup fails. Logging in through a signer advertised in NIP-05 requires the returned account to match the resolved identity, and read-only fallback is unavailable when no signer is found. The pairing acknowledgment goes out once the client is listening, so the client receives the first request.

A new [in-app developer inspector](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4593) for Android and desktop recomputes event IDs and verifies signatures. It converts [NIP-19](/en/topics/nip-19/) identifiers and looks up kinds. Users can also find which relays hold an event and send raw REQ and COUNT messages as well as raw EVENT messages. The inspector answers AUTH only after the user confirms the relay and challenge. The account also needs the user's confirmation first. Its crawls respect blocked relays and Tor routing and stay outside the account cache. Manual testing covered the desktop version only, so the Android build and the AUTH path remain untested. [Fallback handling for unknown kinds](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4592) caches unsupported non-ephemeral events by ID or replaceable coordinate and shows them as a generic card. Cached JSON is capped at 32 KB and a nesting depth of 32. It also holds at most 50 tags, and notifications exclude unknown kinds. The card is a generic display, separate from full support for those kinds. Separately, a [native crypto replacement](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4586) moves Apple and Linux native builds to in-tree Kotlin code derived from BearSSL. That code covers AES and GHASH as well as long-input SHA and HMAC. Android and JVM builds keep using JCA. The change migrates code; constant-time behavior and end-to-end security would each need separate verification.

Concord now [deduplicates and rekeys its community list by authenticated material ID](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4574), fixing a crash from duplicate keys in the hub and a tombstone/leave mismatch that arose when older rescue entries disagreed. [Stricter coordinator-key validation and cordn.net links](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4573) require full 32-byte keys, so the app stays up when given partial input. The same change parses cordn.net chat links and Android deep links while ignoring preview metadata; on Android 12 and later, users enable the deep links manually. A [notification-service startup fix](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/pull/4597) keeps the service from restarting Android's 10-second foreground deadline when it is already in the foreground. While the UI is in front, it starts the service with startService and falls back to a foreground start if the platform refuses. The cold-start crash fix was confirmed in a single test on one SM-T220 device, so behavior on other devices is still unverified. Like the rest of this entry, these changes are merged source awaiting a release.

Amethyst's [MLS](/en/topics/mls/) engine [has been split out of Quartz](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/mls-kmp/commit/9a20715eca4b2e8432da529678425f6be8652735) into a standalone library, [mls-kmp](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/mls-kmp). It is a pure Kotlin Multiplatform implementation of RFC 9420 (cipher suite 0x0001) with [Marmot](/en/topics/marmot/) policy hooks and Welcome interoperability tests against OpenMLS/MDK and ts-mls. The library contains no Nostr logic, which stays in Quartz's Marmot binding. It now reuses a newly extracted crypto-kmp primitives library.

### MDK

MDK, the runtime for Marmot encrypted groups, adds retained files and host handoff leases to its [streaming-media source](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2353). It replaces an arbitrary 2 GiB cap with full-disk blocking and moves legacy migration and storage management to the background. A durable retention cursor protects unread messages, and a state-only reorg keeps messages and output intact. Deferred epoch stalls now wake from persisted state, and quarantine isolates broken groups. A consumed last-resort KeyPackage keeps its `init_key` private material for up to 24 hours after replacement, bounded by `not_after`. A neutral host deployment configuration replaces the implicit application preset.

[Merged avatar media-store conversion](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2527) sends avatar and group-image fetch, read and background migration through media-store cache files. Verified identical files are shared, so only new files use free space. If a file is missing or corrupt, it is cleared for every owner that shares it and fetched again. An owner that changes during a read follows the current asset. When the disk is full or free space is unknown, migration waits and does not delete anything to make room. `cache_bytes` is exposed in the bindings. This builds on [avatar file-cache storage](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2504), which adds deduplication, a 128 MiB budget for unique files, reference counting and encrypted-key cleanup, and stayed backwards compatible until the runtime conversion. [Pre-ship migration 0114](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2524) copies at most 32 MiB of database avatar bytes, smallest first. Owners beyond that cap are evicted from the cache and fetched again later; no messages or history are deleted. This limits the extra disk needed when an account opens. The 21 October nightly predates all three changes, so none of them is in it.

[Merged epoch-gap fallback](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2500) serves relays lacking [NIP-77](/en/topics/nip-77/) by paging events older than the newest 256. Its bounded, durable frontiers survive cuts and capacity waits, so a missing older commit can be found and newer messages decrypted. [Merged catch-up scheduling](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2503) runs up to four slots that refill immediately. It persists complete pending plans and retries refused or skipped goals, while eight NIP-77 permits per endpoint are shared across the SDK pool with cancellation and deadlines. Operational work excludes windows past retention, and history completeness remains unproven.

[Merged QUIC broker TLS verification](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2331) uses bundled Mozilla roots on all platforms, avoiding an Android OS-verifier JNI panic with CA-signed brokers. The trade-off is that OS-managed distrust, custom enterprise CAs and revocation checks drop out of the path, and brokers on a private CA need an explicit certificate pin. An ALPN mismatch on the production broker had previously masked the crash, so universal runtime success remains unestablished. [Merged trust-bundle update](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2516) moves to rustls-webpki 0.103.16 and webpki-roots 1.0.10 to address compromised emSign/eMudhra intermediates in the bundled trust, updating it for both QUIC and relays with no added ignores. Independent cryptographic and runtime exploit validation is still outstanding.

Merged source is not a release. Related tags are dated nightly or snapshot experimental bindings, and stable 0.14 is still unreleased. Supporting source: [own-leaf component refresh](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2292), [recipient-compatible message edits](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2299), [self-authored agent edits](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2300), [host-authorized conversation deletion](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2306), [quarantine receive isolation](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2327), [deferred quarantined projections](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2328), [retained encrypted media](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2329), [persisted epoch-stall recovery](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2343), [wire download checkpoints](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2349), [state-only reorg recovery](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2350), [KeyPackage retention grace](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2352), [streaming downloads](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2353), [profile edit completion](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2382), [staged streaming uploads](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2384), [attachment handoff leases](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2398), [storage management](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2410), [background attachment migration](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2467), [unread-message retention](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2469), [bounded reconciliation](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2490), [explicit neutral deployment configuration](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2493) and [database disk-space reclamation](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2496).

MDK has two open proposals. The first, [Kind 3 follow-list editing](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2529), changes only the target `p` tag when a follow is added or removed. Unrelated tags and content stay intact, as do petnames and relay hints. Signing and publishing stop once a list reaches 2,048 distinct follows. That includes exactly 2,048, because a list of that size is indistinguishable from a longer one. When the current follow list is unavailable, the operation publishes nothing. The CLI runs the same edit under an account lock. Mute-list cap and onboarding paths still need separate fixes.

[Ordered offline catch-up](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/2513): Older recovered ciphertext is admitted before newer MLS state advances, so messages aren't lost when they fall out of the retained ratchet window. When NIP-77 is refused it pages the original routes. It saves unresolved ranges, spill data and cursors so they survive a reopen. Admission and backoff are bounded, and completed catch-up results reach the application before the whole pass finishes. Recovered invites become a durable GroupJoined before any network I/O. The new migration 0115 exists in source only, and nothing is claimed as released.

### Zeus

Zeus is the mobile Bitcoin and Lightning wallet. A set of Zeus pull requests tightens NWC payment handling. Now `pay_invoice` uses its own backend result, so unrelated payments or refreshes in the shared transaction store cannot decide its outcome. Pending payments reserve the invoice amount plus the available routing-fee cap against the budget, and unlisted holds last for the lifetime of the HTLC. Callers paying with ecash must supply the invoice they reviewed, so a quote replaced by a concurrent NWC or send flow cannot melt a different invoice. LNURL ecash navigation also keeps the invoice that passed verification. Authorization checks expiry against the current time, and deleting the active wallet removes its NWC connections and keys. Malformed `make_invoice` amounts or expiries are rejected before native conversion could crash iOS or wrap values on Android ([payment status](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/4513), [zero-amount invoices](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5106), [payment result isolation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5136), [ecash quote binding](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5137), [budget fee holds](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5138), [expiry and revocation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5144), [merchant QR validation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5161)).

Switching the selected embedded or NWC wallet advances a connection generation, so results from the old target cannot fill in the new one. LndHub routing fees now count toward NWC spending, although that backend still has no fee-limit support. [NIP-47](/en/topics/nip-47/) `list_transactions` reports pending and failed payments correctly, and zero-amount invoices receive the requested amount. CLINK checks invoice amounts in millisatoshis and refuses onion relays by normalized hostname. [NIP-05](/en/topics/nip-05/) onion identifiers return not-found without a clearnet lookup, which does not add Tor NIP-05 support. Scanned merchant QR codes no longer send secrets or legacy NWC strings to external resolvers ([onion NIP-05 handling](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5188), [CLINK relay validation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5211), [wallet target generation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5213), [CLINK invoice amounts](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5215), [verified LNURL invoice](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5217), [LndHub spending fees](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5228), [invoice numeric bounds](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5233)).

Two more unreleased source changes continue this work. Embedded LND payment-stream events are now matched by payment hash, so when an NWC service payment and a user payment overlap, each keeps its own final outcome and preimage. Listener cleanup and EOF and keysend timeout handling support the same fix, and AMP payments skip this matching on purpose. Native stream-level errors still lack a payment hash, so they reject all pending listeners together ([payment-hash correlation](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/4351)). Zeus also moves to Alby SDK 8.1.1 and sends `make_invoice` amounts directly as rounded whole millisatoshis, which preserves fractional-satoshi input and meets the SDK's stricter integer checks ([SDK 8.1.1 invoice amounts](https://github.com/ZeusLN/zeus/pull/5296)).

### Safebox Acorn

[Safebox Acorn](https://github.com/trbouma/safebox-acorn), a Nostr-backed wallet component, adds an optional library API for sender-paid fees on confirmed direct ecash relay transfers. In [commit be112f5](https://github.com/trbouma/safebox-acorn/commit/be112f5b0a0743ff0fd5faeec8ee6cb863f97bb5), `quote_ecash_transfer` checks mint input fees and picks denominations covering the net recipient amount plus the receiver's redemption fee; the spend path revalidates that quote before spending. [Commit 19b4f9f](https://github.com/trbouma/safebox-acorn/commit/19b4f9fc69d140d6b61aaf2c33e98d7b1f295301) makes cancelled relay-coordinated wallet-lock acquisition release only the local process lock it owns. Added unit tests cover cancellation at each acquisition stage.

### Applesauce

Applesauce is the JavaScript Nostr toolkit. Merged changes require the exact secret for unpinned NIP-46 pairing and make NWC deadlines cover capability negotiation. They also add a negentropy codec that is safe for large integers and a stricter structural guard for events ([event structure validation](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/86), [NWC negotiation deadlines](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/94), [NIP-46 pairing authentication](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/97), [bounded negentropy integers](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/101)). A redaction pass that removes secrets and plaintext from debug logs is still an open proposal in [debug-log redaction proposal](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/104).

Source commits ([optimistic verifier](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/954adecafd99d30a52b6afdd6ac95fea100a4986), [trusted monitor liveness](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/f816ca8b556d1c6d449a2deb479b5708b70a51f5), [loader outcome routing](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/commit/f8eafecf2cde092c3c19dcc820d47be870bd1f74)) add an optional `OptimisticEventVerifier`. It always checks the event hash but only checks a sample of signatures. That sample drops toward 10% after 10,000 verified events over 14 qualifying days within a 30-day evidence window. Callers must opt in. This is not full cryptographic verification, so authentication and payment paths should keep strict checks. Liveness data from trusted [NIP-66](/en/topics/nip-66/) monitors and loader outcomes now feed relay routing. A new `skipped` value in `SyncRelayState` means apps with exhaustive switches need updates. A changeset requests minor version bumps, but no published release has been confirmed.

An open [fix for `filterTimelineByMutes`](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/105) targets a reversed filter that keeps only muted events. With the fix, the filter would remove events from muted pubkeys, words, hashtags and threads. The bug is reproduced in a unit test, but there's no release or integration proof yet.

Open proposal: [a reworked COUNT request scheduler](https://github.com/hzrd149/applesauce/pull/106) would start with up to 8 requests in flight and double that limit while the scheduler is saturated. Slow responses and timeouts would no longer count as congestion, but errors and rate limits would still halve the limit and trigger a cooldown. It adds a new timeout variant to the `CountFailure` union, which would break code that handles every variant of that type. In the author's own live test against three relays, 120 counts had a median time of about 1.1s, against 2.5–6.5s before. That is one author benchmark, not an independent or general performance result.

Operator listeners that receive full signed events now need a new registration signed by the target, plus fresh read authority. Legacy registrations keep working for 30 days. Operators also gain issuer allowlists stored in the database and mesh JWKS. Hosted mobile onboarding is opt-in, and hosted features turn on once auth domains are configured. A [WebSocket client header option](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8159) lets a client send a project [NIP-FI](/en/topics/nip-fi/) assertion in the connection upgrade alongside NIP-42 AUTH. [NIP-CL](/en/topics/nip-cl/), [NIP-FI](/en/topics/nip-fi/), [NIP-AP](/en/topics/nip-ap/), [NIP-FA](/en/topics/nip-fa/) and [NIP-AR](/en/topics/nip-ar/) are Buzz project specifications that sit outside the accepted NIP set. Sources: [Bring synced Solo chats and projects to mobile](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8337), [keep cached state with its community](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8349), [store per-community NIP-FI issuer bindings](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8372), [account onboarding, key recovery and hosted reconnect](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8375), [resolve NIP-FI issuer bindings from the database](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8377), [support explicit mesh JWKS transport](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8382), [define agent-key assertions in NIP-FA and NIP-FI](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8384), [tag every membership notice with its channel type](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8406), [start a channel with its members in one create](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8421) and [wait for the peer's Close before dropping any WebSocket](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8426).

Later merges tighten relay membership and read scope. Buzz now [writes the NIP-43 membership list in the same transaction as each membership change](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8482) ([NIP-43 topic](/en/topics/nip-43/)), including owner boot and transfer. The relay signs this kind 13534 list, and a failed list write rolls back the membership change. Live fan-out after the commit can still fail while the stored list stays correct. When several updates land in the same second, timestamps are bumped forward one at a time, so bursts can push them ahead of the clock. [Broad channel reads now use a fixed allowlist of channel types](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8469): stream, forum, DM and workflow. Future types appear only when a request names or recognizes them. A #h or #d filter on state kinds is handled as a non-legacy request with access checks, and system #t/#P opt-in applies only within system scope. Push delivery [first reused the read path's channel scope](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8507). A follow-up then [allowed system-channel push only for an explicit #h filter](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8517) naming a readable channel the subscriber belongs to, and broad filters stay excluded. Current clients leave these channels unnamed, so their behavior stays the same. In the message, forum and comment builders, [mention tags must be 64-character ASCII hex](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/6372). Tags are normalized for case and deduplicated, and one malformed member rejects the whole list. The check covers format alone, without testing whether the key lies on the curve.

Two fixes are proposed but not merged. [Scoping each live REQ filter on its own](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8519) would fix two bugs. Channel state queried by `#d` loaded on the first fetch, but later live updates were dropped. A subscription that mixed a channel filter with a community-wide filter also lost messages. Under the proposal, each filter matches only the topics it is authorized for, so it can't pick up access from another filter in the same REQ. Separately, [atomic channel-settings updates](https://github.com/block/buzz/pull/8516) would write the kind 9002 request, every setting change and the related system messages in one database transaction. A failure would return an error and the client could resend. Today, switching a channel from public to private can fail while reporting success, and the retry is then rejected as a duplicate. Live cache updates and subscription revocation would run only after the transaction commits.

### Conduit

Conduit's source now includes experimental occurrence commerce, added in [occurrence assignments and durable merchant acceptance](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/636). Each merchant-owned kind 30410 product assignment is tied to an exact market and date. When a merchant accepts an order, stock is debited once, atomically, and the exact order identity is kept. Rescheduling updates only unused allocations, and existing orders keep their original terms. Under [centralized app policy with additive coverage](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/687), publishing also covers app and personal relays while keeping frozen destinations and signed bytes. A third registry search endpoint is still pending, and executable authority is absent.

Follow-ups build on this. [Event commerce checkout and recovery routing](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/685) routes checkout and organizer stock handout. It also routes recovery. Accepted orders and recovery checkpoints are stored encrypted per account and device. Exact retry envelopes are stored with them. Restoring on a fresh device re-encrypts them, and portable checkpoints reject device-storage markers. [Participant-bound order conversations](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/700) separate local messages and replies by order ID and by the buyer–merchant pair. Payment evidence is separated the same way, which keeps records apart even when a signed order ID is reused. Wire IDs and encrypted history are preserved, and ambiguous legacy links stay unselected. [Bounded anonymous checkout endpoints](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/698) cap authentication and signing requests at 16 KiB as each request streams in. Tip requests get the same cap. The first chunk over the limit cancels the request, while valid split UTF-8 is kept. Existing signer and authorization rules still apply.

Verified wallet phrase and membership recovery now works when only some relays respond ([recovery with partial relay availability](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/683)). Optional encrypted name and approval records keep local progress ([custom-address approval and activation](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/656)). Event discovery batches all followed authors ([discovery across all followed authors](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/617)). Unsigned creation drafts that were blocked from publishing can still be recovered ([blocked organizer draft recovery](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/681)). All of this is merged source only. Release inclusion, deployment, deployed edge and external services, and funded end-to-end operation remain unverified.

Open [PR #682](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/682) would have Conduit write every new Market order as one Open Markets numeric-v2 envelope. Complete order terms travel in a versioned extension. The PR fixes [issue #623](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/issues/623) and builds on [merged participant-bound conversations](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/700). It removes the buyer-facing compatibility selector and the format switches based on features or size. Common fields must agree with the extension, and unknown or conflicting extensions are rejected. The encrypted transport size limit is still enforced, without truncation or switching formats. Multi-option shipping, pickup and routed-payment orders require explicit support declared on the exact merchant-signed listing revision. Existing named and numeric-v1 orders keep their original writers, invoices, signed delivery bytes and recovery state. Version 9 decode rules recover that history without rewriting signed envelopes.

In [the same proposal](https://github.com/Conduit-BTC/conduit-mono/pull/682), direct messages inherit order context only through authenticated references and reply ancestry. Merchants can explicitly resolve missing fulfillment as digital or shipping. That decision is bound to the original parties and terms, and the order itself stays unchanged. Replaying verified historical inventory acceptances leaves stock debited once. The PR remains open and undeployed. Its required E2E aggregate still fails on an unchanged 404 timeout, and funded-settlement and device qualification are still outstanding.

### Wisp

Wisp is the native Android Nostr client. Two Wisp security proposals harden checks on relay events. [PR #679](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/679) covers Nostr Wallet Connect (NWC), whose standalone relay connection bypassed RelayPool's signature gate and matched responses by `e` tag alone. A response would complete a pending request only when it is signature-verified, authored by the connection's wallet-service pubkey and `p`-tagged to the client. The kind-13194 info event needs local signature and author checks before its `encryption` tag is trusted, since a hostile relay could otherwise steer negotiation with a fake one. NIP-04 remains the fallback when negotiation fails. The author calls this defense-in-depth: forging response content still requires the wallet-side ECDH key, so no demonstrated forgery exploit is closed.

[PR #678](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/678) targets spoofed zap receipts. Wisp trusted the sender, message, amount and relays in a kind-9735 receipt's unvalidated `description` tag, letting a self-authored receipt show any zapper and amount in notifications, per-note zap lists, live-stream chat, DM receipts and zap-poll votes. A shared `parseValidatedZapRequest` validator applies NIP-57's client-side checks: the embedded request must be a signature-valid kind 9734 addressed to the receipt's recipient with matching `e`-tag presence and value, the invoice's description hash must equal the SHA-256 of the exact request string, and an amount counts only when the invoice matches the request's signed `amount` tag. Invalid receipts fall through the existing `sats > 0` and null-zapper gates with no call-site changes. This ties invoice to request and leaves open whether the receipt author is the recipient's authorized zap endpoint and whether payment settled.

Both [proposals](https://github.com/barrydeen/wisp/pull/679) are open, unmerged and unreleased. The author reports that physical Android regression tests passed, which we have not reproduced; real-wallet end-to-end testing and integration testing of duplicate and late NWC responses are pending.

## Protocol and Spec Work

### NIP-01 makes empty-list filters match nothing

The [empty-list filter change](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2504) said an empty list matches nothing. An omitted field still places no constraint on that field. Network-wide relay conformance is not established here. On the relay side, [cagliostr's empty-filter fix](https://github.com/mattn/cagliostr/pull/33) makes empty `ids`/`authors`/`kinds` and tag lists match nothing across REQ and COUNT. This also applies to live subscriptions, on both SQLite and PostgreSQL.

### NIP-01 discourages empty-list requests

The [follow-up clarification](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2511), merged 20 October, keeps that MUST rule, removes conflicting text saying lists hold one or more values, and says clients SHOULD NOT send empty lists.

### NIP-59 recipient-deleted gift wraps proposal

An open [gift-wrap deletion proposal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2509), created 19 October, says relays SHOULD refuse to store kind 1059 event IDs again after they are deleted under the recipient `p`-tag deletion rule. This would stop a rebroadcast from undoing a recipient's deletion. The wire format stays the same, and the requirement is SHOULD-level. [NIP-62](/en/topics/nip-62/) already covers rebroadcast after a request to vanish.

### Napplet gamepad interface

The [GAMEPAD draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/108), merged 15 October, makes the runtime the only reader of physical controllers. The focused, visible napplet gets live values. Background napplets get neutral axes, buttons and timestamps but can still see controller connection information. This optional domain uses a subscribe and state-snapshot protocol. Enforcement depends on the host blocking native controller APIs. Its reference implementation points to a napplet-soy feature branch, so broad shipped support is not established.

### Napplet motion interface proposal

An open [MOTION draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/124), created 16 October, gives the runtime ownership of device sensors. The runtime would ask for user consent and send live samples only to the focused napplet. Subscribers can still see availability, permission and focus state. Native sensor APIs are denied only where the host allows it, so isolation depends on the host.

### Napplet location interface proposal

Under the open [LOCATION draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/125), also created 16 October, the default permission grants coarse position only. Precise location and background delivery each need a separate grant. A visible napplet with a grant receives positions. A hidden napplet receives them only if it also has the background grant. No shipped implementation is claimed.

### Napplet mesh interface proposal

An open [mesh domain draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/122), created 15 October, lets napplets publish, subscribe and count blobs through people who have deliberately paired with each other, with no relay or internet connection required. Its `ttl` counts trust-circle edges, not physical transport hops. The runtime handles pairing, routing and deduplication. A napplet picks a hop budget within a cap the user sets. Strangers on the same radio transport are not circle members, and publishing to Nostr networks remains a separate action. The draft makes no implementation guarantee.

### Napplet navigation interface proposal

The open [intent navigation draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/126), created 17 October, has the runtime keep history, visit IDs and the current position for each authenticated napplet instance. Napplets report committed locations for the intents they advertise. Back and Forward send the stored invocation to the same live instance. Delivery alone does not move the position. The napplet's next report is linked to the `IntentDelivery.id`. Top-level and browser-back policy belong to the shell, and native History API or hash changes are not reported automatically.

### ContextVM interface proposal revision

New commits landed on 14 October in the [ContextVM bridge draft](https://github.com/napplet/naps/pull/31), which has been open since June. ContextVM carries MCP over encrypted Nostr messages. The revision isolates pending requests, close handling, events, client identity and payments for each authenticated napplet endpoint. Users can deny a request or require a prompt. They can also allow it within an allowance, and budget checks hold when requests run concurrently. Tools now match by portable per-tool `schemaHash` values and provider pins, which replace shell-local names. Timeouts range from 1 to 300000 ms, with 30000 as the default. The author describes a partial kehto implementation. Its conformance cases are requirements, not passing runtime tests.

### Marmot existing-group component activation

Existing groups can now adopt new components under [Marmot's component activation rules](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/439), merged 15 October. Each client updates the support list in its own authenticated LeafNode. A group becomes eligible only when every canonical leaf advertises the component. An authorized, explicit activation Commit then installs the initial GroupContext state and the required component ID in one atomic step. Upgrading apps does not trigger activation. After activation, a member cannot drop required support and stay in the group. KeyPackages without that support are rejected for additions. No shipped client support is claimed.

### Marmot last-resort init-key grace

Under [Marmot's init-key grace rule](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/440), merged 16 October, clients SHOULD keep a last-resort KeyPackage's `init_key` after confirmed replacement so delayed Welcomes can still decrypt. The recommended window ends 24 hours (86400 seconds) after confirmed replacement or at `Lifetime.not_after`, whichever comes first. They MAY delete it earlier once they stop accepting them. Deletion rules for consumed packages that are not last-resort are unchanged. A compromised old key could open recorded Welcomes during the retention window, which extends exposure by at most 24 hours. It does not give general access to future epochs.

### Marmot account-sync idea

A non-normative [account history and backup design](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/437) was merged 15 October. Under this idea, sibling devices would send read-only history and attachment keys to each other in bundles sealed to the recipient, never MLS state. Small state such as read positions and mutes would travel with them. Backups would use a separate recovery key and would not restore membership. Imported history depends on the sending device and cannot later be authenticated independently through MLS. Expired messages are left out, and attachment availability still depends on external storage. The design creates no implementation requirement.

### Marmot multi-device account proposal

An open [multi-device specification proposal](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot/pull/442), created 17 October, would move the lifecycle of devices and KeyPackage slots into normative text. Each installation would get one random, persistent `d` slot, with no hardware-derived ID. An account could provide at most 10 fresh valid packages per invitation. Leaves would represent an account's devices. Removing a person removes all of that person's leaves in one Commit, and removing a single device remains valid. An optional client tag is self-reported metadata and plays no part in validity or identity. Using [NIP-09](/en/topics/nip-09/) to delete slots is cleanup, not cryptographic revocation. Relays may ignore those deletions. The diff uses spec language about itself, but the proposal is unmerged. Pairing and device-group protocols are still separate design work.

### NWC get_budget method

The optional [NWC-13 get_budget draft](https://github.com/nostr-wallet-connect/nwc/pull/7) ([NWC-13 topic](/en/topics/nwc-13/)), covered previously as a proposal, has now merged into main, on 20 October. It builds on NIP-47. Its permission is separate from `get_balance`, and it reports only the requesting connection's allowance. The request takes no parameters. Responses give `used_budget` and `total_budget` in millisatoshis and a `renewal_period` of daily, weekly, monthly, yearly or never. A `renews_at` field is required for renewing budgets and omitted for never. An empty `{}` result means no budget is configured, which is different from a configured limit of zero. Wallets advertise the method in wallet info and in authorized `get_info` methods, and SHOULD advertise extension 13. The method cannot create or change budgets, and an allowance does not prove that the wallet holds the funds. No wallet implementation is claimed.

### NIP-01 atomic subscription replacement proposal

[An open NIP-01 proposal](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2514) would let a `REQ` that reuses an existing subscription ID replace the whole old filter set atomically at the source. The swap is lossless: events before the `REQ` match the old filters and events after it match the new ones, with everything matching the old filters flushed before any later `EOSE` or `CLOSED`. The initial query may return duplicates, which clients remove by event ID. A refused replacement produces a `CLOSED` that also ends the old subscription, and the client receives nothing further, `EOSE` included, until a `REQ` is accepted. The change needs no new message types or prefixes. It adds no NIP-11 fields either. The author's local measurements across five relays are unreplicated and describe those tests only, separate from current network-wide behaviour. This proposal is separate from the [empty-filter-list clarification](#nip-01-discourages-empty-list-requests).

### Concord forum presentation rules merged

[Concord PR #24](https://github.com/concord-protocol/concord/pull/24) is merged into the spec. It gives CORD-03 an optional ChannelMetadata `view` field that selects chat or forum presentation. Clients must fall back to chat whenever `view` is absent. The same fallback applies when its value is unknown or unimplemented. Chat Plane key and address rules stay the same, and so do its folding rules. `view` uses the existing wire transport with no new cryptographic derivation.

In the [merged rules](https://github.com/concord-protocol/concord/pull/24), a kind 9 `subject` tag makes a titled post limited to 256 UTF-8 bytes; receivers truncate an overlong displayed title and keep the message. Clients must preserve `view` when they update metadata, which keeps a chat-only client from switching a forum back to chat. This is a spec merge, and an app release or wide implementation has yet to be shown.

### Concord roles and voice proposal

[Concord PR #25](https://github.com/concord-protocol/concord/pull/25) is an open proposal for Roles v2, which would rank permissions and allow per-channel and per-category overwrites, with private-channel key distribution tied to VIEW_CHANNEL. Restricted channels would use writer-address keys and writer certificates. The proposal also covers moderation and nicknames, along with channel categories. For voice, it proposes a peer-to-peer iroh mesh that is relay-only by default, with signed presence and moderator directives; its transport ALPN is `concord/voice/1`.

[PR #25](https://github.com/concord-protocol/concord/pull/25) remains open and unmerged. Nothing from it has been released. Its Vector widescreen reference implementation and live tests are the author's claims, which we have not independently checked. The PR body says the work is planned for Vector 0.5.0. Voice interoperability is explicitly incomplete: Vector's peer-to-peer transport and Armada's brokered transport can't share a call yet. CORD-08 (disappearing messages) is excluded, and the evidence falls short of showing Discord feature parity or support across all clients.

## NIP deep dive: NIP-37 and NIP-48

[NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md) and [NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md) set rules on either side of publication. NIP-37 stores an unsigned draft in encrypted form before the author publishes it, and NIP-48 labels a signed Nostr event whose content originated on another network. Both specs are marked draft and optional. They are separate mechanisms: neither requires the other, and neither supplies the other's privacy or provenance properties.

### NIP-37: Draft wraps

[NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md) defines kind 31234 as encrypted storage for an unsigned draft of any other kind. The draft's JSON string is encrypted with NIP-44 to the signer's own public key. Its ciphertext goes in `content`. The `d` tag sets the addressable coordinate, so a later save under the same identifier replaces the earlier wrap. Its required `k` tag names the draft's kind, and a [NIP-40](/en/topics/nip-40/) `expiration` tag is recommended. A blank `content` signals that the draft was deleted. Kind 1234 checkpoints hold the same kind of encrypted payload and point to their parent with an `a` tag of the form `31234:<pubkey>:<identifier>`, which gives a revision history.

Kind 10013 lists the relays where a user prefers to keep private events such as draft wraps. Its `relay` entries are private tags: JSON-stringified, NIP-44-encrypted to the signer and stored in `content`, with the public tag array left empty. Clients must publish kind 10013 to the author's NIP-65 write relays and should send kind 31234 events to the relays it lists. The spec recommends that these storage relays require [NIP-42](/en/topics/nip-42/) authentication and serve events only to the authenticated signer ([NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md)).

Decrypting `content` requires the author's private key ([NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md)).

These properties set the privacy limits. NIP-44 hides the draft body, leaves the outer pubkey, timestamp, kind and tags readable, and a relay can still copy the event. Authenticated download is a recommendation for relay operators that a client cannot enforce. Expiration and replacement signal a change in the stored draft; blank content signals deletion. Earlier ciphertext can remain in relay storage or archived copies. A kind 10013 event on public write relays keeps its relay URLs encrypted but shows that the list exists and when it changed. Every draft and checkpoint is encrypted to the same key, so a compromised key exposes all of them ([NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md)).

### NIP-48: Bridged events

[NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md) lets any event kind carry a tag of the form `["proxy", <id>, <protocol>]`, which indicates the event originated outside Nostr. The `<id>` must be universally unique regardless of protocol. The spec lists four protocols and allows more to be added: `activitypub` with a URL, `atproto` with an AT URI, `rss` with a feed URL plus a GUID fragment, and `web` with a URL. Clients may use the tag to reconcile duplicates bridged from other networks or to link to the source object. The spec defines no bridge registry, deduplication algorithm or trust policy.

The following [kind 1 event](https://njump.me/nevent1qqs9tystwk9ec7chs49ku02yu6sz4q73edy7zgn7wk3sgfk749x5evspz4mhxue69uhhyetvv9ujumt0wd68ytnsw43q92j5a3), created in August 2023 and retrieved by ID from relay.mostr.pub and nos.lol, is the same event NIP-48 uses as its ActivityPub example. It is a historical illustration and is reproduced unchanged.

```json
{
  "kind": 1,
  "id": "55920b758b9c7b17854b6e3d44e6a02a83d1cb49e1227e75a30426dea94d4cb2",
  "pubkey": "79c2cae114ea28a981e7559b4fe7854a473521a8d22a66bbab9fa248eb820ff6",
  "created_at": 1691091365,
  "tags": [
    [
      "proxy",
      "https://gleasonator.com/objects/8f6fac53-4f66-4c6e-ac7d-92e5e78c3e79",
      "activitypub"
    ]
  ],
  "content": "I'm vegan btw",
  "sig": "a72f12c08f18e85d98fb92ae89e2fe63e48b8864c5e10fbdd5335f3c9f936397a6b0a7350efe251f8168b1601d7012d4a6d0ee6eec958067cf22a14f5a5ea579"
}
```

The `id` is the hash of a fixed serialization of pubkey, `created_at`, kind, tags and content, so JSON key order has no effect on the `id` or `sig`. The `proxy` tag names an object URL on gleasonator.com and the protocol `activitypub` ([NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md)).

The signature proves that the holder of the pubkey shown signed this event, including its `proxy` claim. For bridged content that signer is usually the bridge. The signature leaves the remote authorship and source-site authenticity unverified. Whether the source still hosts the object, and whether its content matches the Nostr copy exactly, also require separate checks. A client seeking more assurance can fetch the source object and decide which bridge keys it trusts. Deduplication is optional, and the tag discloses the claimed origin to every reader of the event ([NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md)).

### Implementation examples

#### Amethyst

[Amethyst's Quartz library](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/tree/6ebf90c95cd6dc960915618dc33f3bb799827a8e/quartz) implements these mechanisms in source.

- The [draft wrap model](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/blob/6ebf90c95cd6dc960915618dc33f3bb799827a8e/quartz/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/vitorpamplona/quartz/nip37Drafts/DraftWrapEvent.kt) requires the signer's pubkey to equal the wrap's outer `pubkey` before NIP-44 decryption. It parses the inner JSON and recognizes blank content as a deleted draft. A draft can contain `e` and `a` tags that reference public events, and the model deliberately offers no relay hints for them, so private storage relay URLs do not leak into hints for those public events.
- The [kind 10013 relay list](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/blob/6ebf90c95cd6dc960915618dc33f3bb799827a8e/quartz/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/vitorpamplona/quartz/nip37Drafts/privateOutbox/PrivateOutboxRelayListEvent.kt) writes new lists with `relay` tags in encrypted content and an empty public tag array. On update it moves public `relay` tags left by older clients into the encrypted content, and on read it accepts both locations for compatibility. The migration does not erase public copies that relays or archives already hold.
- The [proxy tag parser](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/blob/6ebf90c95cd6dc960915618dc33f3bb799827a8e/quartz/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/vitorpamplona/quartz/nip48ProxyTags/ProxyTag.kt) and its [tag helpers](https://github.com/vitorpamplona/amethyst/blob/6ebf90c95cd6dc960915618dc33f3bb799827a8e/quartz/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/vitorpamplona/quartz/nip48ProxyTags/TagArrayExt.kt) parse and assemble proxy triples, define constants for the four listed protocol codes, enumerate every proxy tag or return the first, and detect whether an event carries a non-empty valid triple. Parsing reads the claim without verifying the remote origin, and the parser alone does not determine how any screen displays or deduplicates bridged content.

#### Notedeck

At commit [`9395544`](https://github.com/damus-io/notedeck/blob/93955445ae1853924b17c0973e3c037015dd19b0/crates/notedeck/src/account/relay.rs#L346), Notedeck implements NIP-37's private relay list. Private relay URLs for kind 10013 are stored as JSON `relay` tags inside NIP-44 content that is encrypted to the account's own key. They are not published as public NIP-65 read/write flags. When parsing, Notedeck decrypts with the account secret and normalizes the URLs. Read-only pubkey accounts get an empty set, and malformed JSON is logged and ignored. [`private_sync.rs`](https://github.com/damus-io/notedeck/blob/93955445ae1853924b17c0973e3c037015dd19b0/crates/notedeck/src/private_sync.rs) passes these relays to a private client that handles local ingest, fan-out, NIP-77 history catch-up and live `REQ`. This covers the relay-list part only, not a full kind 31234 draft UI. No independent runtime or interop test was run.

#### Mostr

In Mostr's [`transmute.ts`](https://gitlab.com/soapbox-pub/mostr/-/blob/99e94a76cc4e088a7cff95656b6b6b8f9c6d3065/src/nostr/transmute.ts#L229) (20 October commit), `toNoteEvent` appends `["proxy", note.id, "activitypub"]` before calling `signEvent(event, note.attributedTo)`. It emits kind 1111 for comments with a parent and reply, and kind 1 otherwise. `note.id` is the ID of the remote ActivityPub object. The tag labels where the event came from. The signature does not prove Fediverse authorship, account control or that the objects are the same.

### Short history

NIP-37 first appears under that number in [`48674e5`](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/48674e563865b1cb7bb3c5c7869d20055446f408) (Vitor Pamplona, 2024-05-30). That commit moved it from [NIP-35](/en/topics/nip-35/); it is not where draft storage was first proposed. NIP-48 began as "Proxy Tags" in [`064a79f`](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/064a79f6142326fa09cda411c15ceca997d37a03) (Alex Gleason, 2023-08-03).

### Using the two mechanisms

[NIP-37](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/37.md) encrypts an unsigned working draft for its author and routes private data, while [NIP-48](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/48.md) puts a visible origin claim on a signed public event. Both remain optional drafts that work independently. NIP-37 protects the author's unsigned draft. For NIP-48, proof of origin stops at the bridge's signature and does not authenticate the external author. A client may keep a future `proxy` tag inside an encrypted draft, though the existing encrypted example leaves its plaintext unknown.
