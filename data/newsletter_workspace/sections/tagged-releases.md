## Tagged Releases

### MDK 0.9.14: fail-closed group history through faster group creation

[MDK](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk) is the Rust development kit for [Marmot](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/marmot), an encrypted group-messaging protocol carried over Nostr. [Version 0.9.12](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.9.12) makes several group-state paths fail closed instead of guessing. A missing fork anchor is now a hard error ([PR #1329](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1329)), a leave proposal is persisted atomically so a crash cannot leave a half-applied departure ([PR #1360](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1360)), and incident replay refuses to guess a format on manifest-less newline-delimited JSON streams ([PR #1140](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1140)). Convergence testing widened at the same time, with retained-history cross-route recovery ([PR #1350](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1350)), cross-adapter convergence assurance ([PR #1372](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1372)), and generalized isolated convergence campaigns ([PR #1357](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1357)). Relay rejection diagnostics are preserved instead of collapsed into a generic failure ([PR #1361](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1361)).

[Version 0.9.13](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.9.13) landed on 18 August with a versioned storage format v2 ([PR #1421](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1421)), operational migration rails, and delta writes replacing live account projection snapshots ([PR #1435](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1435)). Invite history catch-up starts immediately on open ([PR #1444](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1444)), account setup sheds relay round trips ([PR #1446](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1446)), and macOS bindings ship in the binding bundle ([PR #1402](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1402)). [Version 0.9.14](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/v0.9.14) followed on 19 August with group-creation polish: founding images pre-upload ([PR #1498](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1498)), KeyPackage batching and prewarming ([PR #1494](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1494)), atomic initial message retention ([PR #1497](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1497)), and profile publication that carries account-owned relays ([PR #1495](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/pull/1495)). [MarmotKit 0.9.14](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/marmotkit-v0.9.14) and [wn-agent 0.9.14](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/wn-agent-v0.9.14) ship alongside the core crate. Two MarmotKit snapshots between 0.9.12 and 0.9.13 extended the binding set: the [15 August snapshot](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/marmotkit-snapshot-f93801a1087bd152c8a37395bb8565089484bf23) added macOS arm64 binaries, and the [18 August snapshot](https://github.com/marmot-protocol/mdk/releases/tag/marmotkit-snapshot-137a386bead83b055bfd8a05704e32073aecd1ea) added generated Kotlin bindings and JNI libraries for every supported Android ABI, each with its own checksum and manifest.

### Divine Mobile 1.0.20: minting a badge without leaving the app

[Divine Mobile](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile) is a short-video client that publishes and retrieves video through Nostr. [Version 1.0.20](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/releases/tag/1.0.20) lets a user mint a [NIP-58](/en/topics/nip-58/) badge, the signed award events described in this issue's first deep dive, and hand it to someone without leaving the app. Tapping a badge on a profile explains what earning it required, which is the part of the specification that usually goes unimplemented because the definition event and the award event are stored separately.

The rest of [the release](https://github.com/divinevideo/divine-mobile/releases/tag/1.0.20) is client work: a light theme, crop, rotate, and flip in the stop-motion editor, drafts one tap from the recorder, caption timing against the video, a feed that de-prioritizes already-watched material, screen-reader support across the editor, recorder, and profile tabs, reduced-motion handling, and account settings that manage a Divine email and password and link or unlink accounts. Deleted videos now leave local state, and bookmarks persist. [Last week's 1.0.19](/en/newsletters/2026-08-12-newsletter/#divine-mobile-1019-tightens-accounts-private-messages-and-publishing) hardened account isolation and private-message validation; badge issuance is a new publishing surface on top of that.

### ClipRelay 0.2.0: pairing a signer with a camera

[ClipRelay](https://github.com/tajava2006/cliprelay) synchronizes a clipboard between devices over Nostr. [Android version 0.2.0](https://github.com/tajava2006/cliprelay/releases/tag/android%2Fv0.2.0) adds `nostrconnect://` QR login, so a user can sign in with a signer app on another phone, and adds camera scanning of bunker URLs, which removes the habit of pasting a secret-bearing string through a messenger. Bunker connection now times out after sixty seconds instead of hanging, and the retry button after a failed Amber login works. [Desktop version 0.2.0](https://github.com/tajava2006/cliprelay/releases/tag/desktop%2Fv0.2.0) carries the timeout and login-tab fixes.

[Version 0.1.4](https://github.com/tajava2006/cliprelay/releases/tag/android%2Fv0.1.4) three days earlier added sensitive-clipboard handling: a password copied from a password manager still syncs, stays out of history on every device, expires from relays in minutes instead of a day, and arrives with its concealed marker reapplied so clipboard managers on the receiving side treat it as sensitive too. The same release fixed a signer-session defect where the session could settle on relays the signer was not listening to, which broke decryption while plain synchronization kept working; session relays are now pinned at pairing and widened with the bootstrap set. A liveness probe that could be satisfied by a locally synthesized end-of-stored-events response now demands a real round trip, which is a useful reminder that an `EOSE` a client generated for itself proves nothing about the relay. [Last week's 0.1.3](/en/newsletters/2026-08-12-newsletter/#cliprelay-013-restores-relay-and-signer-connections-after-idle-periods) restored connections after idle periods; these two releases move to credential handling and pairing.

### Bark 1.3.9: a browser signer that runs on Android

[Bark](https://github.com/forgesworn/bark) is a browser extension that provides the [NIP-07](/en/topics/nip-07/) `window.nostr` interface, the object a web page calls to request a signature or an encryption operation. [Version 1.3.9](https://github.com/forgesworn/bark/releases/tag/v1.3.9) declares Android support for the Firefox build, so the add-ons listing installs on a phone. Firefox on Android implements no windows API, so every approval that opened a popup window would have been denied outright; the approval surface now falls back to a foreground tab where dismissing denies, a review action foregrounds it, and the background closes it once the request settles. The release notes record verification on a Pixel 10 Pro XL running GrapheneOS with Firefox 153.0.4, and state plainly that Chromium on Android compiles out the extension subsystem, so no Chromium-derived Android browser can run Bark at all.

[Version 1.3.8](https://github.com/forgesworn/bark/releases/tag/v1.3.8) fixed a NIP-46 interoperability defect in the other direction. Bark probed for a compact Heartwood signing dialect by sending the event as a JSON object, which strictly typed signers including `nak` and bunkers built on rust-nostr cannot parse and silently drop, so signing hung. The probe is now sent only to signers that identified themselves as Heartwood, and every other signer receives a standard `sign_event` call from the first signature onward.

### Bray 3.0.0 and Toll Booth 6.0.0 move to a shared wallet-connect library

[Bray](https://github.com/forgesworn/bray) and [Toll Booth](https://github.com/forgesworn/toll-booth) both pay through [NIP-47](/en/topics/nip-47/) Nostr Wallet Connect, the specification that lets an application request payments from a wallet over encrypted Nostr events. [Bray 3.0.0](https://github.com/forgesworn/bray/releases/tag/v3.0.0) and [Toll Booth 6.0.0](https://github.com/forgesworn/toll-booth/releases/tag/v6.0.0) each declare a breaking change adopting [nwc-kit](https://github.com/forgesworn/nwc-kit) for wallet payments, and Toll Booth removes its payer credential flow in the same change. Both publish reproducible builds whose output was byte-identical across two independent runners, with the tarball hash printed in the release notes so a reader can verify the registry artifact.

Three Toll Booth patches followed: [6.0.1](https://github.com/forgesworn/toll-booth/releases/tag/v6.0.1) pins the negotiated deploy host key, [6.1.1](https://github.com/forgesworn/toll-booth/releases/tag/v6.1.1) pins `cashu-ts` to the version its patch targets, and [6.1.2](https://github.com/forgesworn/toll-booth/releases/tag/v6.1.2) restores an image build.

### NoorNote 1.3.4: joining encrypted communities from an invite link

[NoorNote](https://github.com/77elements/noornote) is a Nostr client for desktop, web, and Android. [Version 1.3.4](https://github.com/77elements/noornote/releases/tag/v1.3.4) adds encrypted Armada and Concord communities as an addon: a user joins through an invite link, sees joined communities listed in settings, and receives activity notifications. The same release adds a control for hiding external quote posts, the highlight notes that quote a paragraph of a web article, either globally or per author, with reposts of them hidden too and the user's own highlights kept visible. Profile resolution was also repaired, so profiles no longer render as a truncated public key or as an anonymous placeholder.

[Version 1.3.5](https://github.com/77elements/noornote/releases/tag/v1.3.5) adds an expander for long notes and corrects the layout of the Armada invite-link input. [Last week's 1.3.2](/en/newsletters/2026-08-12-newsletter/#noornote-132-moves-article-discovery-into-the-social-graph) moved article discovery into the social graph; community membership is a separate surface.

### Mostro moves dispute chat off gift wrap

[Mostro](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro) is a peer-to-peer trading daemon whose orders and messages travel as Nostr events, with [mostro-core](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core) as its shared library and [Mostro Mobile](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mobile) as its client. [Mobile 1.3.2](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mobile/releases/tag/v1.3.2) migrates dispute chat from [NIP-59](/en/topics/nip-59/) gift wrap, the metadata-hiding envelope, to a kind `14` chat envelope, adds the envelope primitives for it, and backs the dispute backlog with durable per-conversation cursors so a cold start does not replay or lose messages.

[mostro-core 0.14.5](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/releases/tag/v0.14.5) fixes a defect in the layer the migration leaves in place: the rumor identifier was not serialized inside the gift wrap ([PR #164](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/pull/164)), which leaves a receiving client without the identifier the unwrapped event should carry. [Version 0.14.4](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/releases/tag/v0.14.4) corrects a ratings bug where the running average folded in the previous vote instead of the new one ([PR #163](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/pull/163)), and [0.14.3](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/releases/tag/v0.14.3) completes the migration to nostr-sdk 0.45.1 ([PR #162](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro-core/pull/162)). [Mobile 1.3.1](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mobile/releases/tag/v1.3.1) switches to Blossom servers that accept encrypted chat attachments and retain blobs indefinitely, and registers push tokens for the child trade keys used by range orders. On the daemon side, [0.18.2](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/releases/tag/v0.18.2) and [0.18.4](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/releases/tag/v0.18.4) are the usable builds; [0.18.3](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro/releases/tag/v0.18.3) is titled as a release not to use.

### NYM 3.73.522: encrypted group chats and an encrypted local store

[NYM](https://github.com/Spl0itable/NYM) is a Nostr client with its own assistant integration. [Version 3.73.521](https://github.com/Spl0itable/NYM/releases/tag/v3.73.521) refines the encrypted group chat implementation and [version 3.73.522](https://github.com/Spl0itable/NYM/releases/tag/v3.73.522) encrypts the local SQLite store, which closes the gap between an encrypted transport and a plaintext cache on the device. [Version 3.73.520](https://github.com/Spl0itable/NYM/releases/tag/v3.73.520) fixes an inline script that broke the content security policy and stops previously seen messages from being presented as new. ### Morganite 0.0.4: verifying a blob before it is cached

[Morganite](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Morganite) is an Android [Blossom](/en/topics/blossom/) server, the media protocol where a file is addressed by the SHA-256 hash of its content and served from any host that holds it. [Version 0.0.4](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Morganite/releases/tag/v0.0.4) verifies a blob's hash during download in a single pass before caching it, which is the check that makes content addressing meaningful on the receiving side. The release also tracks cache size incrementally instead of rescanning the directory on every save, moves blocking network calls onto input and output threads, reuses Tika instances for MIME detection, and persists logs to a local database.
